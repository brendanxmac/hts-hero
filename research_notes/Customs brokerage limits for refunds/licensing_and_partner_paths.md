# Licensing and Partner Paths for a Solo, Unlicensed Tariff-Software Founder (US, as of 2026-10-16)

Scope: practical legal routes for offering refund, drawback, PSC, protest and CAPE (IEEPA refund) help to importers. Primary sources (CBP, CFR, US Code) are marked as such; company and blog pages are secondary and marked [secondary].

Framing fact for every section: by statute, "customs business" explicitly includes payments of duties "and the refund, rebate, or drawback thereof", and no one may conduct customs business "(other than solely on behalf of that person)" without a broker license. The statute has no attorney exemption, and it excludes "the mere electronic transmission of data received for transmission to Customs." — [19 U.S.C. 1641(a)(2), (b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1641) (primary). The regulation's definition also excludes "corporate compliance activity" and mere electronic transmission. — [19 CFR 111.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.1) (primary)

## 1. Becoming a licensed customs broker (individual)

### Takeaway
It's cheap (under about $1,000 in CBP fees) but slow and uncertain. The CBLE is given twice a year, recent pass rates run 12–30%, and the license application, background check and permit come after the exam. Missing the 2026-09-16 registration deadline means the earliest exam is April 2027, so realistic time to first filing is about 12–24+ months. That excludes building ABI connectivity, a bond and a filer code, which are separate steps.

### Cited Findings
**Exam dates and format**
- The CBLE is given "the fourth Wednesday of April and October." The next exam is **October 28, 2026**. Registration opened 2026-08-17 and closed **2026-09-16 at 4:30 PM ET**, and self-selection began 2026-10-02. No 2027 dates are posted yet. — [CBP CBLE notice page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/license-examination-notice-examination) (primary)
- The exam has 80 questions. Examinees scoring 57 or more out of 80 (within 3 questions of passing) can appeal. — [CBP CBLE notice page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/license-examination-notice-examination) (primary)
- The passing grade is "75 percent or higher" (60 of 80), and the exam must have been passed within the 3 years before applying. — [19 CFR 111.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.11) (primary)
- Recent pass rates before appeals: **April 22, 2026: 22%**, October 22, 2025: 12%, April 23, 2025: 30%. — [CBP CBLE notice page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/license-examination-notice-examination) (primary)
- The April 2026 figure is corroborated, and takers reported answer-recording discrepancies (CBP said these were resolved before grading). Recent changes also limit appeals. — [NCBFAA / International Trade Today, May 2026](https://ncbfaa.org/news-advocacy/ncbfaa-in-the-news/2026/05/18/cbp-reports-22--april-broker-exam-pass-rate-amid-answer-recording-issues--appeal-concerns) [secondary trade press]
- CBP has said the 20-year average pass rate is about 20%, ranging from 3% to 65%. The October 2024 exam pass rate was 24%. — summarized from search results citing CBP; see [CBP NGF broker modernization paper, Dec 2024](https://cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/ngf_broker_modernization_issue_paper_-_dec_2024.pdf) (primary; figure not independently re-verified on the page)

**Eligibility**
- An individual must be a US citizen on the date of application, not a federal officer or employee, at least 21 at application, "of good moral character", and have passed the exam (75%+) within the prior 3 years. — [19 CFR 111.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.11) (primary)

**Fees (CBP fee page)** — [CBP Customs Broker Fees](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/fees) (primary)
- Exam fee: **$390**
- License application: **$300 individual**, $500 partnership, association or corporation
- National permit application: **$100** one-time, paid with the first year's permit user fee
- Annual national permit user fee: **$190.88** (the page lists this figure as effective October 1, 2026)
- Triennial Status Report fee: **$100** every 3 years
- Fingerprint processing: $10 per applicant, plus the FBI fee (set separately)
- Floor of CBP fees for exam, license, permit and year-1 user fee: about $390 + $300 + $100 + $190.88 + $10 = **~$991**, before FBI fees, prep courses, bond and software (inference from the figures above).

**National permit (2022 modernization rule)**
- A national permit issued under 111.19 "will constitute sufficient permit authority for the broker to conduct customs business" throughout the US customs territory. — [19 CFR 111.2(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.2) (primary)

**Continuing education**
- The June 23, 2023 final rule requires **36 CE credits per triennial period**. The first period runs **Feb 1, 2024–Jan 31, 2027** and was reduced to **20 credits**, with certification due Feb 1, 2027. The full 36 applies to all later periods. The requirement doesn't apply to brokers who haven't held a license for the entire triennial period, so brokers licensed in 2024–2026 owe nothing until the 2027–2030 period. — [C.H. Robinson advisory, Nov 2024](https://www.chrobinson.com/es-ar/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2024q4/11-04-24-client-advisory-cbp-continuing-education-requirements-licensed-customs-brokers) [secondary]; [STR Trade Report](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/november/customs-brokers-can-start-accumulating-ce-credits-beginning-jan-1) [secondary]
- CBP's own CE page: "Complete your 20 hours of Continuing Education (CE) credits by January 31, 2027." — [CBP Continuing Education](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/continuing-education) (primary)
- A credit is the unit of CE, and the minimum is half a credit for 30 minutes. — [19 CFR 111.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.1) (primary)
- Instructor or speaker credits are capped at 12 per triennial period. Non-government providers must be approved by a CBP-selected accreditor, while US-government training needs no accreditation. — [19 CFR 111.103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.103) (primary)
- The triennial period starts Feb 1, 1985 and every third year after. — [19 CFR 111.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.1) (primary)

**License issuance timeline**
- CBP's publication: about 60 days after notice of passing, the candidate gets an application instruction letter. The application must be filed within 3 years of the pass letter. Review has three levels, starting with a multi-agency background investigation. — [CBP Publication 3001-1222, Customs Broker Licensing Process (Dec 2022)](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/assets/documents/2022-Dec/CBP%20Publication%20%233001-1222%20-%20Customs%20Broker%20Licensing%20Process.pdf) (primary)
- Historically, licensing took 12 months or more after passing, sometimes up to 2 years. A CBP pilot aimed at 90 days. — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/streamlining-broker-licensing) [secondary, dated circa 2012]

### Inferences
- **Realistic schedule for this founder:** the October 28, 2026 registration has closed, so the earliest exam is April 2027 (fourth Wednesday, about 2027-04-28, not yet confirmed by CBP). Results usually take weeks to 2 months, the application letter arrives about 60 days later, and background review takes several months. **First license is about late 2027–2028 at best** if he passes on the first try. With pass rates of 12–30%, plan for possibly 2+ attempts, which is 2028+.
- An individually licensed broker can transact customs business under a national permit without forming a corporation. Under 111.11 and 1641(b)(3), only an entity needs the corporate license and a licensed officer. A licensed individual operating as a sole proprietor appears permissible, but I didn't fetch the trade-name rules (verify 19 CFR 111.30 and 111.19).
- A license alone doesn't let him file. He still needs an ABI connection or software, a filer code, and for drawback a bond and privileges (see sections 4–5). He'd also take on "responsible supervision and control" duties and recordkeeping under part 111.

### Gaps
- No 2027 exam dates have been published by CBP (as of fetch).
- The exam format details (duration, open-book references allowed, remote proctoring) weren't stated on the CBP page I fetched. Check CBP's "General CBLE Information" page.
- I didn't find current (2025–2026) data on background investigation and license issuance times. Contact the CBP Broker Management Branch or NCBFAA.
- I couldn't fetch the 19 CFR 111.19 (national permit), 111.30 (status report) or 111.102 (CE) text directly. eCFR redirected to a block page.

## 2. Hiring or partnering: can an unlicensed company employ a broker and conduct customs business itself?

### Takeaway
No. An unlicensed company can't conduct customs business for others just by hiring a licensed broker. The company itself needs a corporate broker license with at least one licensed officer. A broker also may not agree with an unlicensed person to do customs business for others if the fees or benefits go to that unlicensed person (the only exception is freight-forwarder referrals). A software company can sell software and compliance or analytic services, and refer to a broker who contracts and bills the importer directly.

### Cited Findings
- A corporation or association needs articles that allow customs business and **at least one officer who is a broker**. A partnership needs at least one member who is a broker. — [19 CFR 111.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.11) (primary)
- Statute: an entity license requires at least one licensed officer or member. If none is in place for 120 continuous days, the license is revoked by operation of law. — [19 U.S.C. 1641(b)(3), (b)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1641) (primary)
- A broker "must not enter into any agreement with an unlicensed person" to transact customs business for others if fees or benefits go to that unlicensed person. The only exception is the freight-forwarder referral rule. — [19 CFR 111.36(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.36) (primary)
- Freight-forwarder exception: the importer must be told in advance which broker was selected. The broker bills the importer directly (unless waived in writing), must execute the POA **directly with the importer of record**, and nothing may block direct communication between broker and importer. — [19 CFR 111.36(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.36) (primary)
- When an unlicensed person who isn't the importer hires a broker, the broker must give the actual importer its bill or a copy of the entry (unless DDP or waived). — [19 CFR 111.36(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.36) (primary)
- A broker's employees may act without their own license only for that broker, with a POA or a statement filed with the Center. — [19 CFR 111.2(a)(2)(ii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.2) (primary)

### Inferences
- **Corporate license route:** HTS Hero (or a new LLC or corporation) could get a corporate license by putting a licensed broker on as an officer. That person must be a real officer and exercise "responsible supervision and control." The corporate application fee is $500 (see section 1). The licensed officer must stay; losing them for 120 days revokes the license. This is real equity or employment, not a sign-off service.
- **Referral or partner route:** the 111.36(b) fee-sharing ban is the key constraint. A per-refund revenue share paid by a broker to HTS Hero for referred customs business looks restricted. HTS Hero is not a freight forwarder, so the 111.36(c) exception likely doesn't apply. Safer structures: (a) HTS Hero charges its own software or analysis fee directly to the importer, and the broker separately contracts with, gets the POA from, and bills the importer; (b) a flat SaaS or data fee from the broker to HTS Hero not tied to customs-business fees (needs counsel review). This is an inference, so get a customs attorney opinion.
- Software that prepares data which the importer or broker reviews and transmits may fit within the "mere electronic transmission" and "corporate compliance activity" exclusions, but where that line falls is a legal judgment.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable published rates for part-time or contract licensed brokers or licensed-officer arrangements. Market anecdotes weren't found in sourced form.
- I found no CBP ruling specifically on software-vendor revenue sharing with brokers.

## 3. Partner models in the market (secondary sources)

### Takeaway
Software or analytics firms that aren't brokers generally prepare the analysis and refund file, and a licensed broker partner (or the importer) files. Licensed brokers and law firms run referral programs that pay referral compensation to CPAs and lawyers. Contingency (no recovery, no fee) and refund pre-funding are common in IEEPA refund marketing.

### Cited Findings
- **GingerControl:** "We are not a customs broker, do not file your entries and do not promise a refund amount." It sorts entries by route and deadline and prepares the refund file, and "our licensed refund partner files it." The partner isn't named, and POA and pricing aren't disclosed. — [GingerControl IEEPA refund guide](https://www.gingercontrol.com/blog/ieepa-tariff-refund-guide) [secondary, company site]
- **Capitol Counsel + Meridian Trade Advisors + Clear Cove Partners (2026-07-15):** Meridian's staff, including licensed customs brokers, calculate refunds and **file on the client's behalf**, with pre-funding through a financing partner. There's no upfront cost and payment only on recovery. — [Capitol Counsel announcement](https://capitolcounsel.com/capitol-counsel-announces-partnership-for-ieepa-tariff-recovery-and-refund-funding/) [secondary, company site]
- **Freight Right** (describes itself as a licensed customs broker) runs an IEEPA refund **partner program** for CPAs, tax pros and lawyers. Partners introduce clients, Freight Right does the work, and partners earn referral compensation when Freight Right's fee is collected. Self-reported stats: 28.5% of entries refunded, median 17 days filing to refund, average $9,057 per entry. — [Freight Right partner program](https://www.freightright.com/ieepa-tariff-refund-partner-program) [secondary, self-reported]
- Law firms (for example Troutman) market IEEPA refund practices. — [Troutman IEEPA Refund Practice Overview, Oct 2026](https://www.troutman.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Troutman-IEEPA-Tariff-Refund-Practice-Overview-October-2026.pdf) [secondary]
- In drawback, licensed brokers sell "managed programs": the broker confirms eligibility, files ruling and privilege applications, arranges the bond, files via ABI, keeps records and handles desk reviews. — [Alliance CHB drawback page](https://alliancechb.com/duty-drawback/how-to-file-duty-drawback/) [secondary]

### Inferences
- Freight Right pays referral compensation to non-broker CPAs and lawyers. That sits in tension with 111.36(b) unless it's structured as something other than a share of customs-business fees, or it relies on a legal theory I couldn't verify. Don't copy this model without counsel review.
- The GingerControl model ("we prepare, licensed partner files") is the closest analog for HTS Hero. The broker holds the POA with the importer and files, and the software company does analysis.

### Gaps
- I couldn't find partner-model details for Flexport refunds, Tarifflo, Gaia Dynamics or Tariff Refund Solutions. Searches didn't return their pages. Who holds the POA, who bills, and fee splits remain unverified for these companies.

## 4. Importer self-filing with software support (PSC, protests, CAPE, drawback)

### Takeaway
Importers acting "solely on [their] own account" need no license. They can file CAPE declarations in the ACE Portal (CSV upload, ACH refund enrollment required), file protests through a standalone ACE protest account or on paper, and file drawback if they become ABI filers with drawback software and a drawback bond. PSCs require an ABI transmission, so a small importer realistically uses its broker for PSCs. A software company can legally prepare files for the importer to submit itself.

### Cited Findings
- Exception: an importer acting "solely on his own account and in no sense on behalf of another," and its regular employees or officers acting only for it, need no license. — [19 CFR 111.2(a)(2)(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.2) (primary)
- **CAPE (IEEPA refunds):** importers of record and the customs brokers who filed for them submit a CAPE Declaration in the ACE CAPE tab, by CSV upload of up to 9,999 entry numbers (entry numbers only). ABI is not used. Refunds are by ACH only, so ACH refund enrollment is required. IEEPA refunds may **not** be requested via PSC. Phase 1 excluded certain AD/CVD, protested, drawback, reconciliation and long-liquidated entries. Expected refunds take 60–90 days after acceptance. About 75,300 declarations covering more than 11.2M entries were filed by 2026-04-26. — [V. Alexander summary of CBP CSMS on CAPE](https://www.valexander.com/?p=3814) [secondary, summarizing CSMS]; [JD Supra / Polsinelli on refund portal opening 2026-04-20](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/ieepa-refund-portal-opens-on-april-20) [secondary]
- CAPE needs an ACE Portal account and a US bank account added for ACH. CBP has issued refunds by ACH since 2026-02-06, and a refund without bank details is rejected with no interest for the delay. — [GingerControl IEEPA guide](https://www.gingercontrol.com/blog/ieepa-tariff-refund-guide) [secondary]
- **Protests:** the importer, its broker or its attorney can protest under section 514 within 180 days of liquidation. Filing is electronic in the ACE Protest module or on paper at the port. — [CBP Protests page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/protests) (primary)
- ACE has a **standalone protest filing capability** for those without other ACE Portal accounts, through a separate application (name, EIN or SSN). — [Federal Register 2016-14003](https://www.gpo.gov/fdsys/pkg/FR-2016-06-14/html/2016-14003.htm) (primary)
- Signers: an agent or attorney-in-fact needs a POA filed with the protest. An attorney in good standing before a state's highest court needs no POA. A broker needs a general customs POA (CF 5291). — [19 CFR 174.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/174.3) (primary)
- **ABI self-filing:** a self-filer must apply to CBP, pass connectivity certification and get an ABI filer code, using ACE-certified vendor software. — [StrixSmart on ABI](https://strixsmart.com/resources/blog/what-is-abi-software) and [TariffLens glossary](https://www.tarifflens.ai/glossary/abi) [secondary]

### Inferences
- The most practical self-serve offers for a small importer are **CAPE declarations** (portal plus CSV, which HTS Hero software could generate) and **protests** (standalone ACE protest account, which HTS Hero could draft). Both need no ABI. HTS Hero would be preparing the importer's own filing, which the importer reviews and submits.
- **PSCs** go through ABI, so a small importer without ABI must have its filing broker submit them. HTS Hero can identify PSC candidates and hand a package to the importer's broker.
- **Drawback** self-filing (ABI certification, certified drawback software, 1a bond, privileges) is heavy for a small importer. A broker-managed program is more realistic.
- Caveat on 174.3: an unlicensed company signing protests as "agent or attorney in fact" for many importers would likely be conducting customs business for others under 1641. The importer should sign its own protest.

### Gaps
- I didn't fetch a primary CBP source stating that PSCs can be filed only via ABI, or that a non-ABI importer can't submit PSCs in the ACE Portal. This is widely understood but not verified here.
- I didn't verify current CAPE phase status as of October 2026 (later phases may cover protested or liquidated entries). Check the latest CSMS.

## 5. Attorney route

### Takeaway
I found no attorney exemption from the broker license in 19 USC 1641 or 19 CFR 111.2(a). The assumption that 111.2(a)(2) exempts attorneys didn't hold up. Attorneys clearly can represent importers in protests (no POA needed per 174.3) and in CIT litigation, and some customs firms also run licensed brokerage affiliates. A software firm working with customs counsel, where counsel is engaged directly by the importer, is a defensible route for protests and refund litigation.

### Cited Findings
- 111.2(a)(2) exceptions: own account, broker employees, marine transactions, in-bond carriers, noncommercial shipments and FTZ activities. **No attorney exception.** — [19 CFR 111.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.2) (primary)
- The statute has no attorney exemption. — [19 U.S.C. 1641](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1641) (primary)
- An attorney who is a member in good standing of a state's highest court can file a protest with no POA, and signing counts as a declaration of authorization. — [19 CFR 174.3(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/174.3) (primary)
- The CBP protest page names "importer, their broker, or attorney" as protest filers. — [CBP Protests page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/protests) (primary)

### Inferences
- Attorneys can handle protests and litigation. Whether an attorney can file entries, PSCs or drawback for clients without a broker license is unclear from the text. Many customs law firms appear to have licensed brokers on staff for that reason (not verified).
- Arrangement for HTS Hero: refer importers to counsel, who engages directly with the importer and bills it. Bar fee-sharing rules (ABA Model Rule 5.4) generally bar lawyers from sharing legal fees with non-lawyers, so referral payments from counsel to HTS Hero are also problematic. HTS Hero's revenue should come from its own software or analysis fee. This rule wasn't researched here, so verify.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced examples of software-to-customs-counsel contracts or pricing.
- I didn't find CBP guidance on attorneys filing PSCs or drawback without a license.

## 6. Drawback-specific requirements

### Takeaway
Drawback is explicitly customs business. Claims must go through a CBP-authorized electronic system (in practice ABI/ACE) within 5 years of import. Claimants can self-file on their own claims if they're ABI filers. Accelerated payment needs a written application to the drawback office plus a bond sized to expected claims, and approval takes about 4–6 months by industry estimates.

### Cited Findings
- "All drawback claims must be submitted through a CBP-authorized system." A claim needs the claimant identification number, a broker ID if applicable, and surety code and bond type only when requesting accelerated payment. — [19 CFR 190.51(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/190.51) (primary)
- Time limit: a complete claim must be transmitted no later than **5 years** after the import date of the designated merchandise. Incomplete claims are deemed abandoned, and up to 18 months' extension is possible for presidentially declared disasters. — [19 CFR 190.51(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/190.51) (primary)
- Accelerated payment: written application (physical or email) to the drawback office. It includes company info, IRS number, the responsible person, bond details, the merchandise, the drawback provisions, estimated drawback for the next 12 months, prior denials or revocations, a signed compliance certification, and a program description with sample documents. — [19 CFR 190.92(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/190.92) (primary)
- The bond must cover the estimated drawback for the bond term. If outstanding accelerated claims exceed the bond, more coverage is required. — [19 CFR 190.92(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/190.92) (primary)
- Industry practice: the drawback bond is activity code 1a, and an import bond (code 1) doesn't cover it. Self-filers need CBP-certified drawback software (described as costly, price not given). Privileges take about 4–6 months, then about 30 days to payment via ACH with accelerated payment. Records are kept 3 years from liquidation. — [Alliance CHB](https://alliancechb.com/duty-drawback/how-to-file-duty-drawback/) [secondary]; [USA Customs Clearance](https://usacustomsclearance.com/process/customs-duty-drawback-what-you-need-to-know/) [secondary]

### Inferences
- A claimant filing its own claims is "own account" and needs no license, but needs ABI filer status and software. Preparing and filing drawback for others is customs business, so it needs a license.
- HTS Hero's lawful role: drawback opportunity analysis (matching imports to exports, estimating potential drawback) handed to a licensed drawback broker or the importer. Any savings figures should be framed as potential ("could recover up to").

### Gaps
- I didn't verify CBP's ACE drawback pages on filer code issuance timelines or software certification costs.
- No primary source covered "non-broker drawback service providers" filing for others. One search snippet mentioned them, but the source page didn't support it.
