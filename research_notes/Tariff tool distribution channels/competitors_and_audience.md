# Tariff Tool Distribution Channels: Competitors and US Importer Audience

Research date: 2026-10-26. Scope: US. Budget-limited pass (about 16 searches). Many competitors in the assignment were NOT verified and are listed under Gaps. Nothing below comes from memory alone. Each item has a source, and vendor self-claims are marked.

## Q1. Competitor distribution map: which channels each competitor uses, with traction signals

### Takeaway
Two distribution patterns dominate. (a) Free public web tools tied to news events: Flexport's Tariff Simulator (2025) and Tariff Refund Calculator (Jan 2026), both on a public subdomain and free. (b) Embedded or integration distribution for checkout and landed cost: Zonos and Avalara through Shopify, eBay and carrier integrations, and UPS through its own checkout. A third channel is new and growing fast with almost no big-vendor presence: MCP servers and AI-assistant connectors for HTS and tariff lookups, built so far by small or indie players. Refund and audit startups (Tarifflo, Tariff Refund Agency, Capital Gurus, GingerControl, Cervo) emerged around IEEPA refunds. Several of them sell through customs brokers or on contingency.

### Inferences
- The proven channel in the 2025-2026 turmoil was a free public calculator released alongside a news event, owned by a firm with a services backend. Flexport did this twice: simulator during tariff announcements, refund calculator before SCOTUS. It upsells into brokerage and claims, not into SaaS seats. HTS Hero's free calculator plus audit fits the same pattern, but it lacks a services backend, so the "find, flag, explain → broker files" handoff is the analog.
- Checkout and landed-cost players (Zonos, Avalara, Global-e, Passport) distribute through ecommerce platforms. They mostly solve outbound DDP checkout, not the US importer's inbound duty exposure, so the Shopify App Store is crowded for checkout-duty apps. A US inbound "what will my supplier shipments cost / tariff alerts on my SKUs" app may be less crowded there, but I did not verify this by searching the App Store.
- Refund startups go through customs brokers (Cervo, Tarifflo) or contingency fees (Capital Gurus). Brokers are the gatekeeper channel for ACE data access.
- MCP and AI-assistant distribution is early and populated by small and indie players with data-freshness caveats. Incumbents are absent. This looks like an open lane for a provider with verified, revision-tracked data (HTS Hero's engine).

### Gaps

No sourced facts are given for these.
- Government free baseline (USITC HTS search, CBP, Trade.gov Tariff Tool) was not fetched.
- No traffic or user metrics were found for any competitor tool (Similarweb not accessed). Flexport's virality is unquantified.
- "Tariff Simulator Pro with alerts" (Oct 2025) appeared once and was not found again. It needs confirmation.
- I did not count Shopify App Store listings for "tariff" or "duty" apps.

## Q2. Audience sizing: how many US importers there are and where they sit

### Takeaway
About 239,000 US companies were identified importers in 2024 (Census, preliminary), and about 97% are small or medium (<500 employees). SMEs bring in about 31% of known import value. CBP's IOR registry is far larger: it deactivated about 4.8M dormant IOR numbers in 2026, which suggests a long tail of occasional or one-time importers. About 14,454 licensed customs brokers act as a concentrated channel. The ecommerce universe is large, but it is not equivalent to importers: about 0.5M active Amazon.com sellers and about 1M live US Shopify stores.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 (prelim):** 239,231 identified US importers accounting for $2,921B of imports; 232,804 SME importers with $914.5B of known value (about 97% of importers, about 31% of value). — [Census 2024 prelim profile](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/Press-Release/edb/2024prelimprofile.pdf)
- **2023 (final):** 242,515 importers, of which 236,045 SME; SMEs had $868.3B of $2,743.0B known value (about 32%). — [Census Profile 2023-2024](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/Press-Release/edb/edbrel2024.pdf)
- Census counts nonemployers and firms with missing employment data as SME. — same source
- CBP deactivated about 4.8M IOR accounts with no entry in the prior year (2026). These are secondary sources; I found no CBP primary document. From July 16, 2026, IORs with no entry in 366 days go to "Inactive for Entry Purposes." — [Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE regulatory insight](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2026/september-17th/cbp-importer-of-record-data-accuracy-begins); [STR](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/july/cbp-to-deactivate-dormant-importer-of-record-numbers)
- From Sept 18, 2026, CBP voids IOR numbers with inaccurate or incomplete Form 5106 data. Small importers whose forms were filed years ago are exposed. — [Federal Register 2026-16911](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp); [Diaz Trade Law](https://diaztradelaw.com/cbp-ior-numbers-form-5106/); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/cbp-importer-crackdown-puts-thousands-of-cross-border-freight-shipments-at-risk)
- June 2026 Executive Order 14411 directs CBP to purge inactive importers and create risk-based tiers. — [Brownstein](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/executive-order-tightens-cbp-requirements-for-u-s-importers-of-record/); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/trump-signs-order-tighten-customs-enforcement/)
- **Licensed customs brokers:** about 14,454 active licensed individual brokers (CBP page, last modified Mar 2024). Older NCBFAA and J.J. Keller figures say about 11,000. An unverified blog estimate gives about 6,200 brokerage firms. — [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/node/77830); [NCBFAA FAQ](https://www.ncbfaa.org/membership/existing-member-faqs/becoming-a-customs-broker)
- **Amazon:** Amazon.com active sellers fell from about 584K (Jan 2025) to about 500K (Mar 2026). Global active sellers were about 1.65M at end-2025 (Marketplace Pulse via secondary sources). Chinese sellers were 50.03% of global active sellers in 2025 and 57% of the roughly 51K Amazon.com sellers above $1M revenue. — [AMZ Prep](https://amzprep.com/how-many-active-amazon-sellers/); [LinkMyBooks](https://linkmybooks.com/blog/how-many-sellers-are-on-amazon); [Marketplace Pulse 2025 recap via Milled](https://milled.com/marketplace-pulse/2025-the-great-compression-lb5MbYIcRoMUdxn2)
- **Shopify:** about 1.0M live US stores (993,399 Apr 2025; StoreLeads 1,070,098 in 2026). About 2.67M "active" US stores and about 3.7-3.8M BuiltWith technology installs use looser definitions. — [Red Stag Fulfillment](https://redstagfulfillment.com/how-many-shopify-stores-exist/); [LinkMyBooks](https://linkmybooks.com/blog/how-many-sellers-are-on-shopify); [Cropink](https://cropink.com/how-many-shopify-stores-are-there)
- **Refund-eligible pool:** importers who paid IEEPA duties (Feb 2025 to Feb 2026) are the refund audience. SCOTUS struck down IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026 (Learning Resources v. Trump). — [US Chamber](https://www.uschamber.com/economy/tariff-refunds-faq-what-small-businesses-need-to-know-after-supreme-courts-ruling); [Helium10](https://www.helium10.com/blog/impact-of-tariffs-on-ecommerce-sellers/)

### Inferences
- The core reachable market is about 230K SME importing companies (Census), not millions. The 4.8M deactivated IORs suggest many more occasional importers, though many are likely individuals or one-offs. For a $19-249/mo SaaS, the roughly 230K figure is the realistic ceiling.
- US-based Amazon sellers who import: with about 500K Amazon.com sellers and over half of top sellers being China-based, US-based Amazon sellers who are their own IOR are plausibly in the low hundreds of thousands at most. This is an inference only; no source gives the importing share.
- Many Shopify stores are drop-shippers or do not import as IOR, so live US Shopify stores (about 1M) overstate the importer audience. The overlap with Census's about 239K importers is unknown.
- Brokers are the highest-leverage channel. About 14.5K individuals, or a few thousand firms, touch nearly all formal entries, and they control ACE data access needed for audits and refunds. Cervo and Tarifflo already target them.
- The 2026 IOR voiding and deactivation creates a fresh pain point and event hook for small importers, similar to IEEPA refunds.

### Gaps
- No source found for a breakdown of importers by type (ecommerce vs wholesale/brand vs manufacturer). Census profiles do break down by industry (NAICS: manufacturing, wholesale, retail) in the full PDF, but I did not fetch those tables.
- No authoritative count of active IORs (filed in the last 12 months) was found; 4.8M is the deactivated count, from secondary sources.
- No source found for the share of Amazon or Shopify sellers who are their own US importer of record.
- No 2025 or 2026 CBP-updated broker count was found, and no verified count of brokerage firms (filers).
- Entries per year and the number of ACE portal accounts were not researched.

## Q3. Channel gaps and partnership/acquisition signals

### Takeaway
The gaps are in channels where incumbents are absent: (1) AI assistants and MCP, which has only indie servers and none from big vendors; (2) the customs broker as a reseller or white-label channel for SME importer alerts and audits, which only refund startups are pursuing; (3) importer-side (inbound) apps on Shopify and Amazon seller ecosystems, where existing apps focus on outbound checkout duty. M&A signals show that trade content and compliance data is what acquirers pay for (Descartes: CustomsInfo, Visual Compliance at about USD 250M).

### Cited Findings
- MCP tariff servers exist only from small or indie developers, with data-freshness disclaimers (see Q1). — [mcpservers.org TariffsTool](https://mcpservers.org/servers/www-tariffstool-com-developers); [Glama tariffmonitor](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/daleweaver1981/tariffmonitor-mcp)
- Shopify-integrated duty tools (Zonos, Avalara via Shopify Markets) target checkout duty and tax for international buyers. — [Zonos](https://zonos.com/shopify-duty-tax-app); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/products/global-commerce-offerings/avatax-cross-border.html)
- Broker-targeted refund tooling (Cervo) and broker partnerships (Tarifflo, Tariff Refund Agency) show the broker channel is being opened for refunds. — [Cervo](https://usecervo.com/resources/ieepa-tariff-refund-guide); [TechBuzz Tarifflo](https://www.techbuzznews.com/tarifflo-leverages-agentic-ai-to-automate-tariff-classification/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/tariff-refund-agency-launches-ieepa-refund-platform-help-importers-recover)
- Descartes bought trade content (CustomsInfo) and compliance (Visual Compliance, about USD 250M), showing that data and content assets carry acquisition value. — [Descartes IR](https://www.descartes.com/about/investor-relations/acquisitions/descartes-acquires-visual-compliance); [MHL News](https://www.mhlnews.com/global-supply-chain/access-global-trade-data-new-products)
- Carriers embed tariff calculators in checkout (UPS Global Checkout) and offer refund services, so carriers and 3PLs act as both distribution and competition. — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/firms-launch-tools-to-help-shippers-measure-tariff-costs); [UPS](https://www.ups.com/us/en/supplychain/logistics-solutions/customs-brokerage/us-customs-tariff-refunds)
- Trade associations publish SME refund education (US Chamber), an under-used co-marketing channel. — [US Chamber](https://www.uschamber.com/economy/tariff-refunds-faq-what-small-businesses-need-to-know-after-supreme-courts-ruling)

### Inferences
- The fastest reach per unit of effort for a small company is likely to come from:
  - an official-quality MCP server or ChatGPT/Claude connector that links back to HTS Hero pages;
  - event-tied free tools, such as an IOR-voiding checker or a Section 301 exclusion-expiry alert (exclusion expires Nov 9, 2026 per the [tariffmonitor listing](https://mcpservers.org/servers/daleweaver1981/tariffmonitor-mcp));
  - a broker referral or white-label program.
- Amazon seller-tool ecosystems (Helium 10, Carbon6, Link My Books publish tariff content) are possible partner channels for reaching sellers who import. No partnerships were verified.

### Gaps
- No Shopify App Store or Amazon Selling Partner Appstore listing counts were found for inbound tariff apps.
- Global-e, Passport and Hurricane Commerce partnership and acquisition details were not researched.
- Altana and Kodiak Hub enterprise and government channel details were not researched.
