# Mobile, Browser, Desktop and Messaging Delivery Vehicles for HTS Hero

Scope note: about 17 searches and fetches, run 2026-10-26. Google Play listing pages would not render through the fetch tool, so Play install counts are thin. I could not reach AppFigures or Sensor Tower data. Note that htshero.com (the subject company) appeared in a search result as the clearest Slack/Teams tariff-alert product.

## Q1: Where does demand for tariff tools show up outside Google search (installs, downloads, store activity)?

### Takeaway
Outside Google, demand is very small. Every US-tariff browser extension found has under 100 users. US-focused tariff apps show no visible ratings or download tiers. The only tariff/customs apps with real Play installs (10K+) are Bangladesh customs apps. Demand for US tariff tools is still overwhelmingly web/search-led, mostly through free web calculators such as Flexport's Tariff Simulator.

**Web competitors that soak up demand** (for context)
- Flexport Tariff Simulator searches by product name or 10-digit HTS and covers Section 232, Section 301 and reciprocal tariffs. — [Flexport](https://tariffs.flexport.com/)
- Other free 2026 web calculators include TariffsTool (says it checked rates Oct 23 2026 against HTS 2026 Rev. 21), LookupHTS, Gateway Lines, AMZ Prep, and ustariffrates.com (which has a tariff tracker and alerts). — [TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/tariff-calculator), [LookupHTS](https://lookuphts.com/), [Gateway Lines](https://tariff.gatewaylines.com/), [AMZ Prep](https://amzprep.com/us-reciprocal-tariffs-calculator/), [US Tariff Tracker](https://ustariffrates.com/tariff-tracker)

### Inferences
- The April 2025 tariff news spike produced a burst of extensions and apps, but none built a meaningful install base: every extension is under 100 users, and the apps have no ratings. The audience for store-based discovery of US tariff tools is tiny. Stores are not where importers look for these tools today.
- The 10K+ Bangladesh customs apps suggest mobile tariff demand exists where brokers and clerks work phone-first. That pattern hasn't shown up for US importers.
- The Alibaba/1688 duty-overlay idea is already taken by two tools (DutyDesk, Tariff Lens), but both have effectively no users. The space is "crowded" by listing count, not by traction.

### Gaps
- No AppFigures, Sensor Tower or AppMagic download estimates were reachable. App Store keyword rankings for "tariff calculator", "HTS code" and "customs duty" were not measured.
- Google Play install tiers for the two US-focused apps could not be fetched (the pages wouldn't render).
- Edge Add-ons, Firefox AMO and Safari extension stores were not searched directly. Nothing indicates tariff extensions exist there with traction.

## Q2: Which vehicles are uncrowded with high reach, and how would each deliver value?

### Takeaway
Messaging-based tariff alerts (Slack, Teams, WhatsApp, Telegram) are essentially empty of competitors. HTS Hero already appears as the clearest Slack/Teams tariff-alert product in search. The browser-extension overlay on Alibaba/1688/Amazon has high theoretical reach (shopping extensions reach millions) but is unproven for this use case. Directories such as Product Hunt and There's An AI For That have almost no tariff tools, so a listing would stand out but would likely send low-intent traffic.

### Cited Findings
**Messaging**
- Search found **no dedicated tariff-alert bot on WhatsApp or Telegram**. The only Slack/Teams match was HTS Hero: alerts by email, in-app, Slack or Microsoft Teams per HTS code and country pair, sent at announcement and again when the change reaches the HTS. — [HTS Hero](https://htshero.com/)
- The free official baseline is CBP's Cargo Systems Messaging Service (CSMS), an email list covering guidance such as Section 232 and Section 301 changes. — [CBP CSMS](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/cargo-systems-messaging-service)
- Other tariff-update feeds exist as web pages, not push channels: OIA Global, AAEI's tariff actions timeline, and Global Trade Alert. — [OIA Global](https://www.oiaglobal.com/tariff-updates/), [AAEI](https://aaei.org/tariff-actions-timeline-and-customs-service-messages/), [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/)
- ustariffrates.com also offers HTS-code tariff alerts; which channels it uses was not confirmed. — [US Tariff Tracker](https://ustariffrates.com/tariff-tracker)

**WordPress / WooCommerce plugins (embeddable distribution)**
- Existing wordpress.org duty plugins include EdgeTariff (duty, tax and landed cost at checkout from HS codes, plus restricted-party screening), Dutify (live duty estimates in the cart; needs an API token), Smart Tariff Display (configurable duty % by category; no reviews), and an EU Import Duty plugin. — [EdgeTariff](https://wordpress.org/plugins/edgetariff-tools), [Smart Tariff Display](https://cl.wordpress.org/plugins/?p=274630), [wordpress.org listing](https://wordpress.org/plugins/?p=326847)
- Active-install counts were not captured. These plugins target cross-border sellers (DDP checkout), not US importers' own duty exposure.

**Directories**
- Product Hunt: the only tariff-calculator maker entry found was Bob Xiong's "Import Tariff Calculator" (February 2025), which matches a product description to an HTS code for rough duty estimates. — [Product Hunt profile](https://www.producthunt.com/@bob_xiong)
- There's An AI For That: GingerControl Classifier (an HTS code finder) was listed Feb 10 2026. It is the only tariff tool found there. — [TAAFT](https://theresanaiforthat.com/ai/gingercontrol-classifier/comment/520985/)
- G2 has no tariff-specific category. The nearest is Global Trade Management, whose criteria (document management, supplier portals, approval workflows) don't fit a self-serve calculator. — [G2 GTM](https://www.g2.com/products/traidego/reviews)
- Capterra lists TariffTel, a misclassification and duty-risk flagging tool. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.in/software/1104077/TariffTel)
- Other listicle-style "best HTS software" pages exist and could be listing targets. — [ustariffrates best HTS software](https://ustariffrates.com/best/best-hts-classification-software)

### Inferences
- **Most uncrowded with real reach for the paid product:** Slack and Teams app directories, and WhatsApp alerts. These fit HTS Hero's alert promise (push delivery when a tariff changes) and have no tariff competitors found. Their reach inside the ICP (small and mid-size importers, brokers) is plausible but unmeasured.
- WeChat is in theory relevant for China sourcing, but no evidence was found of tariff tools there, and building for it from the US would be hard. Treat it as low priority.
- **Extension overlay:** competitors' near-zero traction suggests the issue is discovery, not the idea. Store search for "tariff" has no strong incumbent, so a well-reviewed HTS Hero extension could rank first quickly. But store traffic for the term looks tiny, so installs would mostly have to come from HTS Hero's existing web traffic, not from the store itself.
- Directories are cheap one-off listings with no strong tariff incumbents. Expect brief referral and backlink value rather than sustained B2B demand. G2 and Capterra matter more for buyer trust once customers exist to review the product.

### Gaps
- No user or install counts for Slack Marketplace or Teams Store tariff apps (none found at all).
- No data on Discord, WeChat mini-programs or SMS tariff-alert services.
- No Substack or beehiiv tariff-newsletter subscriber counts were researched. RSS and iCal of tariff effective dates had no competitor evidence either way.
- AppSumo, Raycast, Alfred, Mac App Store and Microsoft Store were not searched (time limit). No evidence either way.
- No 2025–2026 Product Hunt launch with upvote counts was found for a tariff tool.

## Q3: Build effort, fees and review times for a solo founder with a Next.js app

### Takeaway
A Chrome/Edge extension that calls HTS Hero's existing API, and a Slack/Teams alert integration, are the lowest-effort vehicles that reuse the Next.js backend. A native mobile app adds store fees, a review loop and push infrastructure, while competitor apps show little demand. A PWA, or no app at all, is the defensible choice.

### Cited Findings
- Chrome Web Store charges a one-time **$5** developer registration fee, paid before the dashboard can be used (since March 2020). — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2020/03/12/chrome-web-store-fee/), [Chromium Blog](https://blog.chromium.org/2020/03/new-developer-dashboard-and.html?m=0)
- Competitor extensions reuse a hosted API: DutyDesk needs an API key from its web app, and Tariff Lens sends only the product title to its classification API. — [DutyDesk](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/cclleafggpkkkjmihkfndmdokoklmgmn), [search summary of Tariff Lens listing](https://chromeboard.com/extension/tariff-lens-afeggbomofalnphnjloioffadkhekinp)
- Competitor app pricing for reference: $2.99/week or $39.99/year (HTS Code & Tariff Calculator), and $19/month Pro (Tariff Lens extension). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744954529), [ChromeBoard](https://chromeboard.com/extension/tariff-lens-afeggbomofalnphnjloioffadkhekinp)

### Inferences
- An extension is a thin client over the HTS Hero calculator API. The work is scraping the product title and seller country on Alibaba, 1688, Global Sources and Amazon, mapping that to an HTS code (the hard part: classification accuracy), and showing the duty stack. That is roughly days to a couple of weeks of work, with a $5 fee. Edge Add-ons can take the same Manifest V3 package.
- Slack and Teams apps reuse the Tracker's alert engine as webhook or bot delivery. HTS Hero already advertises this, so the gap is directory listing and approval, not building.

### Gaps
- The Apple Developer Program fee (widely known as $99/yr), the Google Play fee ($25 one-time), Edge, Firefox and Safari fees, and review times for every store were **not verified with a source in this session**. Treat them as background knowledge only.
- Slack Marketplace and Teams Store review times and requirements were not researched.
- WhatsApp Business API per-message pricing and Telegram bot costs were not researched.
