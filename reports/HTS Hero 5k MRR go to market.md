# Close six partners, then let search compound

_Researched 2026-11-03, the day the 7-day no-card reverse trial shipped. Built from five research notes in `research_notes/HTS Hero 5k MRR go to market/` (revenue math, calculator SEO/AEO, audit and alerts SEO/AEO, broker and forwarder sales, importer channels and the tariff calendar), plus PRICING.md, CORE_OFFERS.md and the October reports in `reports/`. Rank checks were run on 2026-11-03 from a browser that may not be US-located, so positions are indicative. Every tariff date in the calendar needs a Federal Register or CSMS check before content is published on it._

**$5k MRR by Feb–Mar 2027 is reachable only as a stretch, and only through about six high-value deals: roughly four broker or forwarder partners and two Managed accounts, on top of about 27 self-serve payers.** Self-serve alone would need about 130 payers, which means 35k–90k real visitors in four months. That is 40–90x today's traffic, and a $1,000/mo budget cannot close the gap. The honest base case is **$2–3.5k MRR by March**, with $5k arriving in March only if the first Managed account and two partners close by mid-January. Two of the four goals as written point at the wrong scoreboard. A Google #1 for "US tariff calculator" against Flexport is very unlikely in the window. "Tariff alerts" has close to no search volume (6 impressions in three months of GSC data). The better targets are page 1 for softer calculator variants, AI-answer citations, and the tracker and news terms that do carry volume. The constraint is conversations, not product: about 30 qualified calls in 15 weeks with no cold calling. So the daily routine is built around three things: triaging every trial within 24 hours, LinkedIn and warm-customer outreach to brokers and forwarders, and publishing within hours of each tariff event so each one turns into sign-ups and upgrades.

## Two of the four goals need a different scoreboard

### Goal 1, "US tariff calculator": aim for citations and soft variants, not a head-term #1

The head cluster is worth fighting over. "Tariff calculator", "tariff calculator usa", "import duty calculator" and "calculate tariff" each get about **4,400 US searches a month**, and "us customs duty calculator" gets 2,400. "Tariff calculator" carries an $8.02 CPC ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/tariff-calculator)). The SERP, though, belongs to Flexport, whose Tariff Simulator is #1 or #2 for every head variant. SimplyDuty, Gateway, Wove, Paidnice, TimeTrex and Reddit follow ([Google SERP, 2026-11-03](https://www.google.com/search?q=us+tariff+calculator&gl=us&hl=en)). Flexport launched its tools with BusinessWire releases and FreightWaves and SDCExec coverage ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/firms-launch-tools-to-help-shippers-measure-tariff-costs); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260120812295/en/Flexport-Launches-Tariff-Refund-Calculator-Giving-Businesses-Early-Insight-Into-Possible-Tariff-Refunds)). **HTS Hero was not on Google pages 1–3 for "us tariff calculator"** in the 2026-11-03 check. Internal GSC had head calculator queries at positions 35–55 on Oct 23. Ahrefs finds that only about **1.74% of new pages reach the top 10 within a year**, and 72.9% of top-10 pages are over three years old ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)). A #1 by March would be an outlier among outliers.

Two signals point to where HTS Hero can win. Perplexity already ranks HTS Hero **#2 for "what is the best free US tariff calculator"** and quotes HTS Hero's own comparison page on Flexport's limits ([Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/search/a88b6fd2-3379-4f15-aea0-230c0d93ea2b)). That same `/compare/best-us-tariff-calculators` page sits on Google page 1 for "section 232 tariff calculator" ([Google SERP](https://www.google.com/search?q=section+232+tariff+calculator&gl=us&hl=en)). Gateway Lines, a mid-size forwarder subdomain, also proves the on-page pattern that earns an AI Overview citation. Its page shows a visible "Updated October 28, 2026" stamp, a policy log of monitored sources, a worked sample result, a methodology section, a 9-question FAQ, and WebApplication plus FAQPage JSON-LD with dateModified ([Gateway](https://tariff.gatewaylines.com/)). Research on AI citations links fresh pages and direct-answer openings to being cited ([DEV/TJ Digital](https://dev.to/alifar/ai-citation-study-finds-direct-answers-and-fresh-content-are-strongly-associated-with-citations-515c)). Bumping the date without a real change gives no lift ([DemandLocal](https://www.demandlocal.com/blog/content-freshness-ai-rankings/)). One cheap fix is waiting: GingerControl's listicle ranks HTS Hero #5 of 10 but still describes it as "free for 20 calculations/month; Starter $14.99/month", which is stale ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/best-import-duty-calculators)). TariffsTool's Oct 29 roundup leaves HTS Hero out entirely ([TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/best-tariff-calculators-compared)).

**What to aim for by March:** page 1 for two or three softer variants ("section 232 tariff calculator", "hts duty calculator", "tariff calculator by country"); the head term moved from 35–55 into the top 20; inclusion in five or more third-party "best tariff calculator" lists; and HTS Hero cited on at least half of ten tracked calculator prompts across Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode. Keep "#1 for US tariff calculator" as the 12–18-month goal. Under the never-hurt-SEO rule, every change to `/duty-calculator` adds sections and markup and never rewords copy that already ranks.

### Goal 2, "US tariff audit / duty audit": the #1 is reachable but small, so win the problems around it

Audit search demand is thin and fragmented. "Tariff refund" gets about 480/mo, and "tariff audit", "customs audit", "post summary correction" and "ieepa refund" are not tracked at all in the keyword set ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/tariff-news); [audit acquisition plan](./audit-acquisition-plan-2026-10.md)). `/tariff-audit` was already **#3 for "tariff audit software find overpaid duty"** on Oct 30 ([audit acquisition plan](./audit-acquisition-plan-2026-10.md)). A #1 for the exact phrase "tariff audit" is plausible, but it would bring tens of clicks, not hundreds. The traffic that converts sits in the problem and deadline questions. Today GingerControl's "PSC, protest, 1520(d) or drawback" explainer wins those ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/recover-overpaid-import-duties)), and so do law firms on IEEPA.

The calendar now favours HTS Hero. CBP's CAPE system has handled most IEEPA refunds, with about $126B sent to Treasury by Oct 2. About **$11.4B sits in finally liquidated entries that CBP won't refund without a court order naming the importer** ([Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/alerts/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-cape-phase-iii-open/)). The two-year §1581(i) window runs from the date of entry, so the earliest suit deadlines arrive **"as early as February 2027"** ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ieepa-tariff-refunds-critical-developments-phase-iii-delays-and-action-steps)). On Oct 8 the CIT certified a nationwide class in *Freestyle World*. Certification is not a refund order ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/getting-duties-back-where-recovery-stands-under-ieepa-section-122-and-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs/)). The search question is moving from "how do I claim" to "am I stuck, and what deadline am I up against", and few pages answer that well. The competition offers free sample audits on contingency: BorderAudit, Tarifflo, and Declaro, which lets the importer keep 80% ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/borderaudit/pricing); [Stackknack](https://stackknack.com/declaro/tools)). HTS Hero's answer is "keep 100% of what you find; your broker files". But HTS Hero has no matching free hook until ES-003 upload ships. PRICING.md still says upload is "coming soon".

**What to aim for by March:** #1 for "tariff audit" and "duty audit software" as a by-product; top 3 for five or more long-tail problem and deadline pages; and cited by at least one AI engine on two of five audit prompts, the target already set in the audit acquisition plan. Copy stays "could / may qualify / up to", and every page ends with "your licensed broker files".

### Goal 4, "Tariff Alerts": nobody searches the product name, so sell it off the news

"Tariff alerts", "tariff monitoring" and "tariff updates" all return no data from seodata.dev. In HTS Hero's own GSC, "tariff monitoring" drew **6 impressions and 0 clicks in three months**, even at an average position of 3.2. The volume is in "tariff tracker" (**1,900/mo, competition 0.05**), "new tariffs" (12,100), "us tariff news" (8,100), "tariff chart" (9,900) and "us tariffs by country" (2,400) ([seodata.dev tracker](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/tariff-tracker); [seodata.dev news](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/tariff-news)). Small alert products already crowd the results: GingerControl Compliance Radar, HTS Watch at $29/mo, and CodeHTS at $99/mo ([GingerControl comparison](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/trade-policy-alert-services-compared-2026); [HTS Watch](https://tariffsapi.com/hts-watch); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10041906/CodeHTS/)). Essentials at $9 is the cheapest paid option on that list.

**What to aim for:** take the uncontested #1 for "tariff alerts" with one honest "tariff alert services compared" page, then put the real effort into a `/tariff-tracker`-style hub and an upcoming-deadlines page targeting "tariff tracker" and "us tariff tracker". Both carry "watch your HTS codes" capture on every row. "Many Essentials" is also the wrong revenue lens: at about $20 ARPA, 100 Essentials payers make $2,000 MRR and need roughly 2,000 activated trials at a 5% conversion rate. Treat Essentials as the entry rung and the event-day upsell, not the MRR engine.

## $5k takes six high-value yeses, not 130 card swipes

Benchmarks rule out a pure self-serve path in this window. No-card (opt-in) trials convert a median **4–6%** to paid, with the 75th percentile at 10–15% ([Userpilot summarising ChartMogul/Poyar](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)). Kyle Poyar's model gives about **0.36% of visitors becoming payers** on a no-card trial. Top-decile early SaaS grows **10–17% a month** ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-report/)), while going from about $100 to $5,000 in four months would take about 165% a month. Bootstrapped no-card trials churn about **6.3% a month** on average ([Startups for the Rest of Us ep. 721](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-721-7-key-takeaways-from-the-2024-state-of-independent-saas-report)). No verified benchmark exists for reverse-trial conversion, so the plan uses 5% as the base and 8% as the stretch. Price level is the lever that matters: in the MicroConf survey, founders with $1,000+/mo entry plans added about **3.4x more new MRR per month** than those priced at $1–9 ([MicroConf](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr)).

The demo-to-close range comes from Optifai (SMB about 32%) and Visionary (SMB horizontal SaaS 9.2%) ([Optifai](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/demo-to-close-conversion-rate/); [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/b2b-demo-conversion-statistics-2026)). Plan for 20–24% of booked demos to no-show ([Starr Conspiracy](https://www.thestarrconspiracy.com/insights/benchmarks/b2b-gtm-strategy-benchmarks-2025)). The other mixes are worse. Pure self-serve needs about 2,800 activated trials. A sales-only mix with three Managed accounts would take about 9–15 founder hours a week in delivery, and losing one client would cost about 24% of MRR. The swap rule keeps the plan honest. **Every Managed deal that doesn't close needs about four partners or 14 Pro accounts to replace it.** If Managed fails its first five calls, the partner line has to carry the plan, and the broker note shows that is possible. Ten partners averaging 20 clients each come to about $549/mo apiece, roughly $5.5k on their own.

The paid budget changes the picture only at the high end. Google Search averages **$5.42 a click and $66.69 a lead** across industries in 2026 ([ppc.io citing WordStream](https://ppc.io/blog/google-ads-pricing-cpc)). LinkedIn awareness runs about $11.24 a click and Reddit about $3.11 ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/article/b2b-paid-awareness-benchmarks-q1-2026)). The 2-month ROI rule caps acquisition cost at about $170 for a Pro account and $660 for a partner. So paid money goes only to exact-match deadline and refund terms that land on the audit or check page, plus event-timed Reddit bursts. LinkedIn ads and the generic "tariff calculator" keyword stay off.

## Brokers and forwarders buy a client impact report, not software

The partner pool is large compared with the goal: about **14,000 licensed customs brokers, about 1,400 NCBFAA member firms and 9,000+ FMC-licensed ocean intermediaries** ([NCBFAA](https://www.ncbfaa.org/about-ncbfaa); [FMC](https://www.fmc.gov/?p=6327)). Their pain is real and on the record. NCBFAA president JD Gonzalez fields dozens of "what do I owe" questions a day and says some automation "has been thrown out". Other brokers redo cleared entries after late rule changes, and some added $1–5 per HTS code in fees because of stacking ([AJOT/Reuters](https://www.ajot.com/news/us-importers-turn-to-brokers-to-navigate-trump-era-tariffs-at-a-cost)). Their filing platforms (CargoWise, Descartes, CustomsCity) process entries; they don't watch each client's products for changes ([GingerControl comparison](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/trade-compliance-software-customs-brokers-2026)). Competitors are small and opaque. US Tariff Rates sells a $499 "Broker AI" portal. TariffDesk, Global Tariff Rates and CargoTrans sell white-label alerts "under your brand and your client relationship" without public traction figures ([CargoTrans](https://cargotransinc.com/tariff-solutions-for-customs-brokers/); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tariff-desk)). At $299 for 10 clients, HTS Hero sits mid-market and is easy to explain.

The fastest buyers are, in order: **(a) existing forwarder and 3PL classification customers** (warm, and allowed to take referral fees); **(b) small independent brokers** with 30–150 importer clients; then consultants and e-commerce 3PLs. The opening offer is a free, co-branded **client tariff impact report**, built during the demo from one real client's product list. It shows full stacked duty, pending changes and possible exposure, and the broker forwards it under their own name. Next comes a **30-day pilot on up to 10 clients** with founder-run CSV setup, then $299/mo with a **founding-partner price lock that ends at the NCBFAA conference (Apr 11–14, 2027, Orlando)** ([NCBFAA](https://ncbfaa.org/membership-benefits/conferences-events)). The three objections each get one answer. Poaching: HTS Hero never markets to partner clients, the terms include a no-solicit clause, and alerts carry the partner's brand. Data: the client list stays in the partner's workspace and can be exported or deleted on request. Liability: HTS Hero is decision support, and the broker reviews and files. Under 19 CFR 111.36, brokers get a flat licence by client count. Brokers never get revenue share or per-entry fees ([Tariff tool distribution channels](./Tariff%20tool%20distribution%20channels.md)). Recurring referral fees go only to non-broker forwarders, 3PLs and agencies.

No major broker event falls between November and March, so the channel runs on LinkedIn plus warm email. Logistics LinkedIn benchmarks show **32.3% connection acceptance and 11.8% reply to messages once connected** ([Expandi](https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/logistics-and-supply-chain/)). At 100 personalised requests a week, that comes to about 30 accepts, about 3.5 replies and about 1.5 booked demos a week. Over 15 weeks that is roughly 20 demos and 4–6 partners at 20–30% close. Warm customers, a "monitor this for all your clients" CTA on the `/hts/9903.*` pages that brokers already search, and one regional association webinar (LACBFFA, NY/NJ, Florida, Houston, Laredo) add the rest. LACBFFA already runs outside-speaker webinars ([CPSC calendar](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/Public-Calendar/2026-08-19-140000/Office-of-Import-Surveillance-Presents-to-the-Los-Angeles-Customs-Brokers-and-Freight-Forwarders-Association)). **Pilots started after about Feb 1 are unlikely to convert before March**, and the holidays slow decisions, so November is the month to fill the pilot slots.

## The operating plan

The plan assumes about 7 hours of GTM work on weekdays from Bangkok (UTC+7). US Eastern business hours (UTC−5) fall in the Bangkok evening, so 8:00–11:00 pm Bangkok is 8:00–11:00 am ET. That evening window is the only one for live calls. US-day news lands overnight and is waiting each morning. The routine in the revenue note was sized for about 20 GTM hours a week; this version scales it to the ~90% focus while keeping its order of priority.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is no longer "how do I rank and convert at scale" but "how do I hold about 30 good conversations before February". Search and AI visibility are worth building now because they compound after March. But the revenue that lands inside the window comes from people who already serve many importers, and from a calendar of tariff events that keeps handing the founder reasons to write to them. The reverse trial is an under-used asset in that plan. Every expired trial becomes a free account with a catalog, so each dated tariff event becomes a sales day for Essentials and Pro without new traffic.

The biggest risk is not a channel failing. It is the founder spending the build block on features while the daily triage and LinkedIn quotas slip. With a 7-day trial and a February pilot cutoff, a lost week in November can't be recovered in March. Treat the 07:00 triage and the 13:30 prospecting block as fixed appointments, and let everything else move around them.
