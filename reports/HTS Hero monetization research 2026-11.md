# How HTS Hero could make money from its traffic

November 18, 2026. Research by eight parallel research agents (market, ads, affiliates, data/API, paid content, ecommerce sellers, customs-business law, AI and small channels), checked against our own Mixpanel and Search Console data. The key third-party claims were re-fetched and confirmed on the source pages (marked ✓). Items marked (U) come from third-party pages only.

Constraint throughout: nothing resembling customs business.

## The short answer

1. **The market agrees with our zero sales.** No free tariff calculator we found makes meaningful money from the tool itself. Flexport, Wove, Ship4wd, GingerControl and TariffsTool all use the calculator as a lead magnet for something else: freight, brokerage, refunds or workflow software. Subscriptions sold to people doing one lookup don't work for anyone.
2. **Our traffic is worth money to the businesses next to the duty result:** paying overseas suppliers, finding and inspecting suppliers, fulfilment, freight, customs bonds and financing. Affiliates and flat-fee sponsors can capture that with no new product.
3. **The best things to sell directly are one-time purchases tied to the lookup.** Examples: a downloadable duty report for the code just looked up, or a duty check on a whole catalog. Nobody in this niche sells these yet, so we'd be testing an idea, with no proof it sells.
4. **The strongest proof of paid self-serve demand in the whole space is Shopify apps.** Sellers outside the US who ship duty-paid to US buyers pay about $10 a month for apps that add a tariff charge at checkout ✓. About half our visitors are outside the US.
5. **Realistic money in the next 3 months: about $500 to $3,000 a month,** mostly from affiliates and one or two sponsors. That's small but real, and it grows with traffic. Bigger amounts would come later from data licensing, a Shopify app or exam prep.
6. **Two legal flags on what we already have.** The Classification Assistant and the entry audits sit on or over the customs-business line under a January 2026 CBP ruling (see Legal).

- Estimate: **$300 to $1,500 a month** now. The affiliate agent estimated $800–3,000, assuming the offers reach ~13k people a month; we show results to ~2.7k a month, so the lower range is safer. A single ShipBob or 8fig deal swings a month.
- No freight forwarder, broker, bond seller or cargo insurer pays cash affiliates publicly. Freightos pays only in shipping credit ✓(U).
- Cost: one tidy "Next steps for this import" box. Keep it off the parts of the page that rank and get cited, or add it below them (never hurt SEO).

**2. Flat-fee sponsor slots: "Featured partner" by category.** Legal: green if the fee is flat.
- Who would pay: customs bond sellers (tariffs pushed bond sizes up 3x to 15x for some importers), payment and FX providers, fulfilment, digital forwarders, freight software, possibly brokers.
- Price benchmarks: FreightFigures, a free duty and bond calculator site, asks $500 / $1,500 / $2,500 a month ✓. But it names only one "founding partner" and every other category says "Sponsorship available" ✓, so there's no proof anyone pays those prices. Maritime Executive's newsletter asks $11,520 a month for a top banner ✓(U). SupplyChainBrain's directory charges $995 a year.
- Estimate: **$300 to $1,500 a month** per category once sold. It takes founder outreach (30–50 emails) and a simple "Advertise" page with our traffic numbers. Usually 1–3 months to the first deal.
- Must be a flat monthly fee. Never per lead, per entry or a revenue share with brokers (see Legal).

**3. A "Buy" test for a one-time Duty Report.** Legal: green (general information on the code the user chose).
- What it is: for the code and country just looked up, a PDF and CSV with the full duty stack and sources, rate history, recent actions, scheduled changes, and the same code from the top 10 countries. $9 to $19, or 3 for $29.
- Evidence: the Carfax pattern (free basic lookup, then a paid report of history for one ID: $45 one report); GPO still sells the free HTS as a $195 printed book ✓(U). No trade site sells per-code reports, so this is untested.
- Do it as a fake door first: a "Download full report" button that records clicks and says "coming soon, get it free when it's ready" with an email field. Two weeks of clicks tells us whether to build it.
- If it works: 0.2–0.5% of result-viewers buying would be ~$50–250 a month now. Small, but it's revenue we control and it grows with traffic.

**4. Free AI programs (an hour in total).** Fill in Microsoft's Publisher Content Marketplace interest form, add an RSL licence file, and watch Search Console for Google's invite-only AI contribution panel. Expect $0–500 a year. Never block or charge AI search bots: they bring the AI Overview citations.

**5. Display ads: mostly skip.** Mediavine Journey would accept us now (1,000 sessions), at about $150–330 a month. Raptive needs 25k page views and long-form pages. Ads add heavy scripts, cost Core Web Vitals and trust, and could show Flexport's calculator next to ours. If cash is needed, test only on `/tariff-updates` and blog pages.

### Next: 1 to 3 months of work

**6. Catalog Duty Check, one-time.** Upload a CSV of HTS codes, countries and values. Get every line's duty stack, flags (Section 232 metal, possible AD/CVD, Section 301 list) and "changed since" date, as CSV and PDF. $29 up to 200 lines, $79 up to 2,000, with a free 5-line preview right on the result page. Legal: green, since the user supplies the codes and we never pick them. It reuses Duty Guard's import flow. Every paid competitor tier sells this (Flexport Pro, GingerLite at $150 a month, Dutify) but as a subscription; nobody sells a one-off.

**7. Shopify app: correct US duty at checkout for duty-paid sellers.** The strongest proven demand found. "Upcharge Surcharge Fee Tariff" has 208 reviews at 4.8 and about $10 a month ✓; "Magical Surcharge" has 143. Merchants use them to add flat US tariff fees. Shopify's own duty calculation charges some codes 100% instead of 10%, and merchants lose orders over it (Shopify Community, Sept 2025 to May 2026). Our edge is the exact stacked duty per HTS code and country. Price $9.99 a month, 14-day trial. Legal: green (the merchant maps codes; we compute). Risks: at least 8 per-SKU tariff apps launched in 2026 with zero reviews, so the winning angle is "accurate duty at checkout", sold to sellers already searching for help. App review and support take ongoing time.

**8. Data licence: rate history plus the tariff actions feed.** For economists, consultancies, research teams and data platforms. Global Trade Alert charges CHF 20–200 a month for individuals and from CHF 3,400 a year for teams ✓(U). Yale's Budget Lab gives effective rates away free, so we'd sell what it lacks: point-in-time Chapter 99 history with sources, exclusions and the structured actions feed. $2.5k–10k a year per team, through direct outreach. One or two deals would beat everything above. 1–3 months to a first deal, if one comes.

**9. Turn the API and MCP back on as a funnel.** At least 10 cheap US tariff APIs appeared since 2025 ($10–199 a month) with almost no sign of demand: TariffsAPI's Apify actor has 3 users. Worth switching on only as a way to find data-licence and platform leads. Expect a few hundred dollars a month at most.

### Later: long-term or small

**10. Customs broker exam (CBLE) prep.** A proven, safe education market. About 2,600 people a year apply for the exam ($390 fee) ✓(U), with pass rates of 12–30%, so many retake it. Prep sells for $325 to $5,999 (ST&R $1,250–1,500, LACBFFA $595). Rough spend ~$1M a year. The exam is open book on the HTS, which is our core asset, and the "HTS Hero" name fits. A practice bank and HTS drills at $79–149 per exam sitting could earn $10–40k a year. It needs its own content and SEO, separate from lookup visitors.

**11. Digest sponsorship.** Worth selling at 3–5k engaged subscribers ($250–750 per weekly send for a narrow importer audience). Until then, SparkLoop's paid recommendations at sign-up pay $2–3 per subscriber, about $50–250 a month.

**12. Flat-fee directory of brokers, forwarders and 3PLs by port.** NCBFAA charges $699+ a year for membership that includes a listing. Flat annual listings only.

**13. Small or slow:** trade compliance job board ($99–299 a post, usually under $500 a month in year one); free embeddable calculator with a backlink (for SEO and leads, since paid demand for widgets is unproven); a Google Sheets `=HTSDUTY()` add-on (none exists; demand untested); selling the site eventually (content sites ~2–2.8x yearly profit, SaaS 3–6x yearly revenue, so recurring revenue raises the value a lot).

### Skip

- **Duty Guard alerts as the main paid product.** Alerts alone sell poorly to lookup traffic everywhere we looked. Keep alerts as a free reason to sign up (free on a few imports), and use paid alerts only as an upsell for heavy users.
- **Paid tariff newsletter.** Law firms give tariff news away free. Paid trade news works only for newsrooms charging companies $400–5,000 a year. No solo tariff newsletter shows 1,000 paying readers.
- **Chrome extensions on Alibaba, Amazon profit-after-tariff tools, paid communities.** Near-zero paid adoption; Helium 10 gives tariff analysis away inside its suite.
- **Selling "which companies visited" data.** Privacy lawsuits under California's CIPA run $2,500–5,000 per violation.
- **Ads inside AI answers, GPT Store revenue.** No real route for a publisher.
- **Refund (IEEPA/CAPE) referrals for a share of the refund, and per-lead deals with brokers, bond sellers, insurers or lawyers.** See Legal.

## Legal: where the line is

Not legal advice. From 19 USC 1641, 19 CFR 111, and CBP rulings read by the research agent.

**Green:** advertising and flat sponsorships (labelled), affiliate links for freight, payments, finance and software (disclosed), data and API sales, general reports, courses including exam prep, newsletters, the calculator on a code the user supplies, alerts on published rates for codes the user supplies.

**Yellow:** customs bond and cargo insurance referrals (state insurance laws allow only fees not tied to a sale; flat sponsorship is the safe form); savings or program detection ("may qualify", requirements explained, the broker decides); a general refund-eligibility explainer (never entry-level decisions or filing help).

**Red:**
- **Any fee from a customs broker that depends on leads, entries or revenue.** 19 CFR 111.36(b) and rulings H276784 and H290002 reject finder's fees and commissions; a flat amount not tied to a transaction is allowed. A per-lead fee hasn't been ruled on and leans risky.
- **Preparing or deciding what goes on an entry, correction, protest, drawback or CAPE refund claim.** Only the importer of record or the filing broker can file CAPE.
- **Classification beyond 6 digits for goods someone intends to import.** CBP ruling H350722 (Jan 16, 2026) says this generally needs a licence, and a disclaimer doesn't fix it. A general classification database with a real disclaimer, available whether or not anything is imported, is "narrowly permissible".

**What this means for what we already have:**
1. **Classification Assistant (`/classify`, $39–89 a month).** It suggests full 10-digit codes for products people import. Under H350722 that is the clearest risk we carry. A competitor's comparison page (GingerControl, Oct 2026 ✓) lists "HTS Hero Classify: Starter $39 a month for 10 classifications; Pro $89 a month for up to 100." Options: stop selling it, limit it to 6 digits, or have a customs attorney review it.
2. **Entry audits (Duty Guard Pro).** Flagging over and underpayments on specific entries is yellow to red, with no ruling directly on point. Taking audits off sale, as already planned, also removes this risk.

## Recommended plan

**Weeks 1–2 (small builds, measurable):**
1. A "Next steps for this import" box under the duty result and on country pages with 3–4 affiliate offers (Alibaba, Airwallex or Wise, ShipBob, QIMA), with disclosure. Apply to the programs first: approvals take days.
2. A "Download full report" fake-door button on the result and `/hts` pages, tracked in Mixpanel.
3. An "Advertise" page with traffic numbers and 3 flat-fee slots, and outreach to 30–50 bond, payment, fulfilment and freight software companies.
4. Microsoft PCM form and RSL file.
5. Decide on the Classification Assistant.

**After 30 days, read the numbers:** affiliate clicks and earnings, report-button clicks, sponsor replies. Then build the winner:
- Report clicks strong: build the Duty Report, then the Catalog Duty Check.
- Many visitors from outside the US clicking: build the Shopify app.
- A sponsor or data buyer bites: double down on outreach.

**In parallel, simplify the offer** as already discussed: alerts free on a few imports as the sign-up reason, cheap paid alert tiers for heavy users, audits off sale.

## Parked idea: free alerts plus one flat $9 plan

Discussed 2026-11-18, parked to come back to. Replaces the tiered alerts plans and the trial.

- **Free:** tariff impact alerts on up to 5 imports, forever. No trial, no credit card.
- **$9 a month:** alerts on all your imports, with a "Buy" button straight to checkout and no trial.
- **Open choice:** $9 unlimited (simplest; raise the price for new customers later) or $9 up to 100 imports and $29 above (keeps room to charge brokers more).
- **With it:** drop the 7-day trial, take audits off sale (also removes the audit legal risk), and add "Email me when this tariff changes" next to every calculator and `/hts` result.

Why: the sign-up button gets a real reason ("tell me when this changes"), and a larger email list is worth more to affiliates and digest sponsors. Flat and cheap is the only pricing with proven traction in this niche (the ~$10 a month Shopify tariff apps).

Expected money: small. At ~90 sign-ups a month and 3–5% paying, about $25–40 a month at first, growing with traffic. The main value is sign-ups.

Work involved: new Stripe prices, plan limits in code (`TRIAL_ALLOWANCE`, `ALERT_TIERS`, `REVERSE_TRIAL`), pricing pages, `PRICING.md` and `CORE_OFFERS.md`, emails and copy.
