# Ship HTS Hero where importers already work

The best way to put HTS Hero in front of more US importers is a short list of channels where the work is mostly done and competitors are thin. They are not the app stores with the biggest headline numbers. **First, switch on the MCP server that already exists in the codebase** (`app/api/mcp/route.ts`, six read-only tools plus a duty-card widget, currently returning 404 until `MCP_SERVER_ENABLED=true`) and submit it to the Claude Connectors Directory and the ChatGPT Plugin Directory. Both platforms suggest tools inside the chat when they're relevant, one endpoint serves both, and no incumbent tariff vendor offers a free, open listing there. **Second, list the existing Slack and Teams alert delivery in those app directories.** That is the one push channel where research found no tariff competitor at all. **Third, reach many importers at once through intermediaries:** co-branded alerts and an embeddable calculator for forwarders and 3PLs, flat-fee software for brokers, and free education slots at SBDCs and trade associations. The rest are real channels but poor bets for a solo founder in the next 90 days. Shopify is the strongest of the deferred ones, and accounting stores, browser extensions, native mobile apps, WhatsApp, Amazon's Appstore and the enterprise agent marketplaces come after it. Their audiences either don't match US importers of record, have shown almost no demand for tariff tools, or sit behind procurement and certification costs. The ceiling matters for sizing every channel: Census counts only **about 239,000 identified US importers, 97% of them small or medium** ([Census](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/Press-Release/edb/2024prelimprofile.pdf)). Millions of Shopify stores and Amazon sellers overstate the real audience by an order of magnitude. Nobody publishes install or conversion data for any channel here, so every ranking below is a judgment based on fit, cost and competition, not on proven traffic.

## The ranked list: twelve vehicles scored for a solo founder

The table scores each vehicle on five axes. **Reach** means how many US importers of record it could plausibly touch, not the platform's total users. **Fit** means how well it carries each of the three products: the free calculator (C), the paid Tracker (T) and audits (A). **Competition**, **effort** and **cash cost** are judged for one person who already has a Next.js app, an API and working email/Slack/Teams alerts. Rank weighs effort heavily, as the brief requires.

The vehicles to defer entirely are native iOS/Android apps, WhatsApp and Telegram bots, the Amazon Selling Partner Appstore, BigCommerce/WooCommerce/Wix plugins, NetSuite, Business Central/AppSource and Odoo, Salesforce AgentExchange, Microsoft Agent Store, Google Cloud and AWS agent marketplaces, Brex/Ramp/Mercury perks pages, and direct integrations with CargoWise, Magaya or Descartes. The sections below explain why the top of the list is where it is and why these fall off.

## AI assistants are the one high-reach channel that is nearly free

The AI directories rank first for three reasons. They have the largest audience of any channel here, they find tools for users without being searched, and HTS Hero has already paid most of the build cost. OpenAI reported **900 million weekly ChatGPT users in February 2026** ([DemandSage](https://www.demandsage.com/chatgpt-statistics/)). It opened app submissions in December 2025, built on MCP ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-now-accepting-chatgpt-app-submissions-from-third-party-devs-launches)). Its guidelines say plugins with strong utility "may be eligible for enhanced distribution opportunities, such as directory placement or proactive suggestions" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)). Anthropic runs **one Connectors Directory across claude.ai, Desktop, mobile, Claude Code and Cowork**. Every listed connector is automatically eligible for in-chat "Suggested Connectors", ranked by usage ([Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/directory)). That suggestion feature is the real prize. Someone asking "what's the duty on HTS 9403.60 from Vietnam" can be shown HTS Hero without ever searching a store. Gateway Lines, the one tariff plugin confirmed in ChatGPT's directory, reports exactly that pattern: users find it by search, by direct link, and by ChatGPT surfacing it on its own for HTS and duty questions ([Gateway Lines](https://gatewaylines.com/news/gateway-tariff-calculator-chatgpt-plugin)).

The competitive picture differs between the two platforms. In ChatGPT, Gateway Lines already holds the free "landed cost by HTS and origin" slot, with Section 301/232 and AD/CVD in its breakdown and no sign-in. Indie MCP servers (TariffsTool, ProfitPather, tariff-resolver, USTariffRates and others) fill the open registries ([mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/www-tariffstool-com-developers); [GitHub](https://github.com/opsloft/tariff-resolver)). The incumbents, Avalara and Flexport, ship MCP servers only for paying customers behind OAuth ([Avalara](https://developer.avalara.com/mcp-servers/cross-border/); [Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/technology/flexport-mcp/)). A search of Anthropic's connector registry during this research **returned no tariff-specific connector**, so Claude looks like an open first-mover slot. Plain duty lookup is becoming a commodity in this space. HTS Hero has to win on what the others lack. The tools already built (`calculate_import_duty`, `compare_origins`, `get_duty_history`, `get_hts_code`, `get_recent_tariff_changes`, `calculate_duty_batch`) already include dated history and origin comparison. Note-accurate Chapter 99 stacking and AD/CVD matching should be stated plainly in the tool descriptions. Both platforms forbid descriptions written to steer the model, so accuracy and clarity are the only levers.

The platform rules decide how far each product can go. ChatGPT **bans selling subscriptions inside a plugin, bans promoting upgrades and bans linking to checkout**. It allows signing in to an existing paid account, allows saying that a feature needs a different plan, and requires that the plugin "must not provide a worse version" than the website. It also rejects "trial or demo plugins" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)). That fits HTS Hero's pricing almost exactly. The calculator is already free and unlimited, so it can run in full with no sign-in. The links in tool output can point to the code page or to free sign-up, in line with the rule that free calculator CTAs push accounts, not the trial. The upgrade to the Tracker then happens on the website. A second phase can add OAuth-gated tools, such as "list alerts on my imports" and "summarize possible overpayments on my uploaded entries". Avalara already ties MCP access to a paid subscription this way. Claude requires OAuth 2.0, tool annotations, a privacy policy and a test account for authenticated connectors ([Claude submission docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)). Audit output in chat must use the same "may qualify / could recover / your broker files" wording as the website. On a Claude Team plan, members can press "Request" on a directory connector, which notifies the workspace Owner ([Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/directory)). That gives HTS Hero a bottom-up path into importing companies where an analyst uses Claude.

One honest caution comes from the earlier SEO and growth reports: **there is still no evidence that these listings drive sign-ups.** No directory publishes installs, and no comparable product discloses conversion from in-chat use. The case for ranking this first is that the remaining cost is about a day of submission work, while the possible audience is far larger than any other channel's. The existing `libs/mcp/track.ts` already records the calling client by name, so referrals can be measured from launch day. The same endpoint also works with Perplexity custom connectors and M365 Copilot through MCP ([hjarni.com](https://hjarni.com/docs/connect-perplexity-mcp.md)). The enterprise agent marketplaces (Microsoft Agent Store, Google Cloud's A2A gallery, AWS, Salesforce) each add a protocol, admin approval or a security review ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/marketplace/docs/partners/ai-agents); [Salesforce](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/isvforce/guide/appexchange-listing-approvals.html)). They only become worth it if a broker or enterprise tier appears later.

## Push alerts and intermediaries carry the paid Tracker

The calculator travels well through AI chat. The paid Tracker needs a channel that delivers a message at the moment a rate changes, and **messaging is the emptiest such channel found.** Research found **no tariff-alert bot on WhatsApp or Telegram and no Slack or Teams tariff-alert app except HTS Hero itself**. HTS Hero's site already advertises alerts by email, Slack or Teams per HTS code and origin pair, sent at announcement and again when the change reaches the HTS ([HTS Hero](https://htshero.com/)). The only other "tariff alerts in Slack" product found serves Mexico (Camtom) ([Camtom](https://camtomx.com/integraciones-apps/slack)). The free official baseline is CBP's CSMS email list ([CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/cargo-systems-messaging-service)). It covers every change for everyone, which is exactly why a filtered "this hits your SKUs: 25% → 50%, +25%" alert is worth paying for. Publishing to the Slack Marketplace and Teams store therefore costs listing paperwork, not engineering. Their review requirements and how many importers browse those directories were not researched, so treat reach as unknown. WhatsApp and Telegram stay deferred: there is no evidence that US importers want alerts there, and message pricing was not researched.

Intermediaries matter because they multiply reach. About **14,454 licensed customs brokers** ([CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/node/77830)) and the NCBFAA's roughly 1,400 member firms handle **more than 97% of US import entries** ([NCBFAA](https://www.ncbfaa.org/about-ncbfaa)). They also hold the entry data that audits need, which no app store API provides. The broker channel is real but contested. TariffDesk sells brokers a multi-client dashboard with white-label client alerts ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tariff-desk?metric=mrr)). US Tariff Rates sells a branded "Broker AI" portal at **$499/month** ([ustariffrates.com](https://ustariffrates.com/broker-ai)). Cervo and Tarifflo use brokers as their refund channel ([Cervo](https://usecervo.com/resources/ieepa-tariff-refund-guide); [TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzznews.com/tarifflo-leverages-agentic-ai-to-automate-tariff-classification/)). The model fits HTS Hero's legal position, because the broker decides and files while HTS Hero finds, flags and explains. It carries one hard constraint from the earlier brokerage-limits work: under 19 CFR 111.36(b), **HTS Hero must not take commissions, percentages or per-entry fees from brokers**. A flat software subscription not tied to any transaction is the safe shape. The earlier growth plan also cautioned that automatic audits can feel threatening to a broker. Brokers should therefore be allowed to self-select through a "for brokers" page and the NCBFAA affiliate route, not prospected cold.

Forwarders and 3PLs are the easier near-term partner. They are not bound by the broker fee rule, so paying them referral fees is fine. They already create tariff content for merchants without building tools: ShipMonk runs an IEEPA refund guide ([ShipMonk](https://www.shipmonk.com/resources/content-hub/ieepa-tariff-refund-guide-2026)), and Kuehne+Nagel publishes an IEEPA how-to ([Kuehne+Nagel](https://www.kuehne-nagel.com/how-to/customs-clearance/ieepa-tariffs)). The large forwarders built their own tools: Flexport's free simulator and refund calculator, C.H. Robinson's Tariff Impact Analysis, UPS Global Checkout ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/firms-launch-tools-to-help-shippers-measure-tariff-costs)). They are competitors, not partners. The mid-market players with importer audiences and no import-duty monitoring are the target: 3PLs, parcel software (ShipStation's duty features only face outbound shipments ([BusinessWire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20251118263606/en/ShipStation-Streamlines-Duty-and-Tax-Payments-for-US-Businesses-Shipping-Internationally))), sourcing agents and small forwarders. The offer packages work already scoped in the growth plan: an embeddable "Powered by HTS Hero" calculator for their sites, and a multi-client workspace that sends co-branded impact alerts to their importer clients.

Inventory software is the third intermediary and the most surprising gap. Katana markets a "Manage tariffs" solution page but names no tariff data source ([Katana](https://katanamrp.com/solutions/manage-tariffs/)). Cin7 users have an open request to "automatically accrue and track tariffs" by country of origin, and say landed-cost allocation works only per whole PO, not per line ([Cin7 community](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/13696284566287-Feature-Request-Ability-to-Automatically-Accrue-and-Track-Tariffs)). Both vendors rank for "tariffs + [product]" without a rate engine. A "plug HTS Hero rates and alerts into your landed cost" integration is therefore a credible business-development pitch, though it takes weeks of outreach rather than a self-serve listing.

## Institutions and news events deliver audits better than any store

Audits are the hardest product to distribute, because they need the importer's entry data: an ACE ES-003 report or 7501s. No Shopify, Amazon or accounting API exposes that data. CBP's CAPE refund tool, live since **April 20, 2026**, works from lists of entry numbers that only the importer of record or its broker can submit ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/04/us-cbp-launches-ieepa-refund-tool.html)). Every store app would therefore still have to send users to an upload on htshero.com. What works for audits is trust plus a deadline. Flexport proved the pattern twice: a free simulator during the 2025 announcements, then a free **Tariff Refund Calculator launched January 20, 2026**, ahead of the Supreme Court ruling, that reads an uploaded ACE entry report and then offers to help with claims ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260120812295/en/Flexport-Launches-Tariff-Refund-Calculator-Giving-Businesses-Early-Insight-Into-Possible-Tariff-Refunds)). HTS Hero has no brokerage to sell into. Its version of that handoff is "keep 100% of what you find; your broker files."

Two current events are ready for the same treatment. From **September 18, 2026, CBP voids importer-of-record numbers with inaccurate or incomplete Form 5106 data** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp)). IORs with no entry in 366 days move to inactive, after about **4.8 million dormant IOR numbers** were deactivated ([STR](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/july/cbp-to-deactivate-dormant-importer-of-record-numbers)). A one-page "is your IOR at risk" explainer and checklist tied to a free account is a fresh hook for exactly the small importers HTS Hero wants. One MCP listing also notes a Section 301 exclusion expiring **November 9, 2026** ([mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/daleweaver1981/tariffmonitor-mcp)). If the HTS notes confirm that date, it is a natural "get alerted before it lapses" Tracker prompt, and the date should be checked against the note text before anything is published.

Small Business Development Centers and trade groups are the credible, free way to put the calculator and audit in front of importers who don't search for them. SBDCs already run tariff sessions for importers: Florida SBDC at FIU on HTS, country of origin, ACE duty tracking and drawback; Illinois SBDC's free "Trade Tariffs in 30 Minutes"; and an SBDC seminar that demos the US Commercial Service FTA Tariff Tool, which shows they already demo third-party tools on stage ([FIU SBDC](https://growbiz.fiu.edu/2025/05/tariffs-and-trade-what-you-need-to-know-now/); [Peoria Chamber](https://business.peoriachamber.org/events/details/trade-tariffs-in-30-minutes-22642); [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/event/79766)). The US Chamber publishes a small-business refund FAQ ([US Chamber](https://www.uschamber.com/economy/tariff-refunds-faq-what-small-businesses-need-to-know-after-supreme-courts-ruling)). A guest session on "find your rate, check for possible overpayments, bring the findings to your broker" costs a few hours. It reaches people with a live problem and suits HTS Hero's unlicensed, educational stance. The weakness is scale: each session reaches dozens of people, not thousands. The recorded session, and the backlink from the host's page, are worth as much as the room.

## The big app stores are real but premature

Shopify is the strongest channel to defer, not to drop. It has the best data of any platform: each inventory item exposes `harmonizedSystemCode`, `countryCodeOfOrigin` and `unitCost`, all readable and writable ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/2026-07/queries/inventoryItems)). An app could, on install, pull every SKU's HTS code, origin and cost, flag missing codes and start watching them, with no CSV import. The economics are generous: a **$19 one-time fee and 0% revenue share on the first $1 million of lifetime revenue** ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/partners/partner-program/glossary); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/32186933/shopify-developers-lose-annual-revenue-share-break-as-program-moves-to-lifetime-model)). Importer-side tariff apps are almost absent. DutyCalc, launched May 2026 with nearly HTS Hero's pitch (SKU-level 301/232/reciprocal/AD/CVD exposure; $49–$199/month), shows **0 reviews**, and no importer-side app found has more than 2 ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/dutycalc)). The demand Shopify merchants have shown went to "tariff surcharge" apps that pass the cost to shoppers, at 94–197 reviews each ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/product-fees)). That is a different job. The empty shelf may mean opportunity, or it may mean merchants don't look for exposure tools in the store. The evidence can't tell those apart. One unverified issue blocks building now: Shopify generally requires its own Billing API for in-app charges, which may clash with HTS Hero's Stripe billing. Check that rule first. If the rule holds, a free app that syncs the catalog into a regular HTS Hero account, with billing kept on the website, may be the compliant design. The earlier growth plan said "skip"; this research narrows that to "not yet, and only after the AI and alert channels show whether outside channels convert at all."

The other stores fail on fit or cost. On Amazon, more than half of top Amazon.com sellers are Chinese, and many ship through a forwarder acting as importer of record ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazons-significant-reliance-on-chinese-sellers)). The sellers who remain already get tariff modeling inside Helium 10's Tariff Analyzer ([Helium 10](https://www.helium10.com/blog/new-tariff-analyzer-tool/)). QuickBooks and Xero have huge SMB bases and no tariff app, and bookkeepers clearly struggle with landed cost ([beancount.io forum](https://beancount.io/forum/t/tariff-accounting-chaos-my-clients-cogs-just-jumped-25-40-and-nobodys-books-are-ready-how-are-you-tracking-landed-costs/3658)). But no source says what share of them import. Intuit now meters API reads, the exact calls a bill-reading monitor would make ([Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/blog/quickbooks-api-pricing-and-the-intuit-app-partner-program)). Xero certification needs live customers and "can take several months" ([Codat](https://www.codat.io/blog/get-your-app-certified-on-the-xero-app-store/)). Business Central and Odoo already carry static US duty calculators sold through implementation partners ([Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/tariff_tracking)). NetSuite charges $3,000 a year just to join its developer network ([NetSuite](https://netsuite.com/portal/developers/sdn.shtml)).

Browsers and phones fail on demand. Every US tariff Chrome extension found has **fewer than 100 users**. DutyDesk on Alibaba and 1688 has 5, and Tariff Lens has 4 to 92 depending on the version ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/cclleafggpkkkjmihkfndmdokoklmgmn); [ChromeBoard](https://chromeboard.com/extension/tariff-lens-afeggbomofalnphnjloioffadkhekinp)). US HTS apps on iOS show no ratings, including one charging $39.99 a year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744954529)). The only tariff apps with 10,000+ Play installs serve Bangladesh customs clerks ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.customstariff&amp%3Bhl=en_US&amp%3Bgl=US)). The April 2025 tariff spike produced a wave of extensions and apps, and none built an audience. For US importers, tariff discovery happens on the web and, increasingly, in AI chat, not in stores. A Zapier/Make app and a Google Sheets `=DUTY(hts, country)` function are the exception worth a week later on. Neither has any US tariff competitor, and a Zapier Community thread shows users scraping government pages to fake one ([Zapier Community](https://community.zapier.com/how-do-i-3/need-help-automating-small-business-projects-using-zapier-51952)). A Sheets add-on should avoid restricted scopes, though, or Google's CASA assessment adds weeks and possibly thousands of dollars ([Switch Labs](https://switchlabs.dev/post/casa-tier-2-tier-3-security-review-providers-pricing-and-the-cheapest-option)).

## Suggested order of execution

**Week 1** costs almost nothing and covers ranks 1, 2 and 7. Add `/api/mcp` and its OAuth paths to the Vercel firewall Bypass rule, set `MCP_SERVER_ENABLED=true`, and confirm that every tool response carries a canonical htshero.com link with a UTM tag. Submit to the Claude Connectors Directory and the ChatGPT Plugin Directory, then publish to the official MCP Registry, Glama, Smithery, PulseMCP and mcp.so. In the same week, submit the existing Slack and Teams alert delivery to those directories.

**Weeks 2–4** build the intermediary layer, ranks 3, 5 and 6. Ship the embeddable calculator widget and the co-branded multi-client alert workspace, and pitch them first to the forwarder and 3PL customers who already pay HTS Hero for classification. Email three to five SBDC international trade centers offering a free session. Publish the IOR/5106 explainer and, if the HTS note text confirms the date, the exclusion-expiry alert prompt.

**Month 2** adds the second phase of the AI channel and the business-development work, ranks 4 and 8. Add OAuth-gated MCP tools for "alerts on my imports" and "possible overpayments in my uploaded entries", worded as "may qualify". Open a "for brokers" page with flat-fee pricing. Pitch Katana and Cin7 on rate-engine integration.

**Month 3** is a go/no-go review, then ranks 9 and 10. Build the Zapier app and the Sheets function. Build the Shopify app only if two things are true: the Billing rule allows the design, and the AI and alert channels have shown that outside referrals turn into accounts with imports. Everything below rank 10 stays parked until a broker or enterprise tier exists, or until real demand appears.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the instinct to chase the largest marketplaces. For a product whose market is about 240,000 importing companies, the channels that matter are those that reach importers when they have a tariff question, or that put HTS Hero inside a business already serving many importers. AI assistants catch the question, Slack and Teams carry the alert, and forwarders, brokers and SBDCs supply many importers at once. HTS Hero has already built the hard part of the first two. That makes the next month unusually cheap, and it can also answer the open question both earlier reports left unresolved: do outside channels convert at all? Measured referrals from the MCP and alert listings should decide whether Shopify and the other stores ever earn a build.

The second insight is structural. Audits, the product with the highest ticket, can't travel through any app store, because entry data lives with the importer and its broker, not with Shopify, Amazon or QuickBooks. Audits will grow through trust channels and through deadline events, Flexport's playbook minus the brokerage. HTS Hero can't file, so its advantage is the honest line competitors can't say: you keep what you find, and your broker files it. Every channel above should carry that message.
