2026 HTS Revision 11 restores the Section 122 exemption for steel and aluminum under heading `9903.82.02`, effective April 6, 2026, so goods entered under that heading from June 8 to June 30, 2026 owed no 10% Section 122 duty. It corrects a typo from Revision 10 that had pulled the main steel and aluminum heading out of the exemption for the three weeks it was in force. Everything else in Revision 11 is statistical.

## What changed in Revision 11

### A typo in the Section 122 exemption, corrected

The 10% Section 122 duty (`9903.03.01`) didn't apply to goods that already paid a Section 232 duty. For metals, U.S. note 2(aa)(v)(1) lists which Section 232 headings qualify for that exemption (`9903.03.06`).

Revision 5 listed "headings `9903.82.02` and `9903.82.04`–`9903.82.17`", and Revisions 6 to 9 extended that to `9903.82.19`. Every version included `9903.82.02`. Revision 10, in force from June 8, 2026, extended the range to `9903.82.26` for its new headings but printed the first heading as `9903.82.04`: "headings `9903.82.04` and `9903.82.04`–`9903.82.26`". That dropped `9903.82.02`, the main 50% heading for steel and aluminum articles, out of the exemption.

Revision 11 restores `9903.82.02`, so the note now reads "headings `9903.82.02` and `9903.82.04`–`9903.82.26`". The change record calls it a technical correction under Proclamation 11021, effective **April 6, 2026**.

<Callout tone="primary" title="The fix covers June 8 to June 30">
Because the correction is dated April 6, before Revision 10 took effect, the law now reads as if `9903.82.02` was never dropped. Steel and aluminum entered under `9903.82.02` from June 8 to June 30, 2026 owed no Section 122 duty. If you paid it, you may be able to get it back.
</Callout>

### Everything else: statistical breakouts

The rest of the change record splits existing lines into new 10-digit statistical numbers in chapters 7 to 94, effective July 1, 2026. For example, `0704.20.0000` is now split into `0704.20.0020` and `0704.20.0040`. These change how you report the goods. They don't change any duty rate.

## Section 122 on 9903.82.02 steel: before and after the fix

**On $10,000 of steel** under [`7206.90.00.00`](/hts/7206.90.00.00) from Vietnam (base rate free), entered under `9903.82.02`:

| Entered | Section 232 (50%) | Section 122 (10%) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 15, 2026, as Revision 10 was published | $5,000 | $1,000 | $6,000 |
| June 15, 2026, after the correction | $5,000 | exempt | $5,000 |
| July 1, 2026 (Revision 11) | $5,000 | exempt | $5,000 |

One related point is worth checking. The note only lists `9903.82.02` and `9903.82.04` to `9903.82.26`. Articles on the metals lists that are entered under `9903.82.01` (no aluminum, steel or copper) or `9903.82.03` (metal under 15% of the weight) don't pay Section 232 metals duty, and they aren't covered by the Section 122 exemption either. Those paid the 10% until Section 122 ended at the close of July 23, 2026.

If you entered `9903.82.02` goods in June 2026, compare your entry summaries against Revision 11 and ask your broker about recovering any Section 122 duty paid.

## What changed after Revision 11

Section 122 expired at the close of July 23, 2026, and the Section 301 forced-labor duties took its place from July 24 ([Revision 13](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13)). Their Section 232 exemption, heading `9903.05.90`, lists the same metals headings, `9903.82.02` and `9903.82.04` to `9903.82.26`. For the full sequence of 2026 changes, see the [2026 HTS revisions hub](/blog/2026-hts-revisions).
