## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Revision** | 2026 HTS Revision 19 |
| **In force from** | September 15, 2026 |
| **Tariff changes effective** | September 15, 2026 |
| **Programs affected** | Section 338 Canada |
| **Previous revision** | [Revision 18](/blog/2026-hts-revision-18) |
| **Next revision** | [Revision 20](/blog/2026-hts-revision-20) |

Revision 19 is all about Canada. The 50% Section 338 duty started on August 22 in [Revision 17](/blog/2026-hts-revision-17). On September 15, Proclamations 11064 and 11065 widened it. Two of the three product lists got longer, and the exemption for Section 232 goods shrank to dairy only. The rate stayed at 50%.

## What changed

### List 51(b)(1), heading 9903.03.12: 63 to 101 provisions

Added:

- 23 cheese provisions, from `0406.10.64` to `0406.90.97`, and `1518.00.40`
- hides and furskins, including `4101.50.10`, `4107.11.50` and `4301.60.60`
- boats under `8903.31.00`, `8903.32.00` and `8903.93.20`

Whisky and liqueurs were narrowed. The full provisions `2208.30.60` and `2208.70.00` came off the list and specific statistical numbers went on, such as `2208.30.60.20` and `2208.70.00.30`. So `2208.30.60.20` still pays, while `2208.30.60.80` no longer does.

### List 51(b)(3), heading 9903.03.14: 439 to 513 provisions

Added (82 provisions), including:

- seats and furniture under `9401` and `9403`, and mattress supports and mattresses under `9404`
- lamps under `9405.21` and `9405.29`
- steel structures under `7308.90` and aluminum bars, profiles and tubes under `7604` and `7608`
- base-metal fittings under `8307` to `8311`, and paper under `4802.61` to `4802.69`
- control panels `8537.10.91.30` to `8537.10.91.70`, and cars under `8703.10.50` and `8703.21.01`
- cheese `0406.90.99` and a boat under `8903.99.21`

Removed: salt (`2501.00.00`), cement (`2523.29.00`), `2940.00.60`, `4803.00.40` and unwrought lead (`7801.10.00`). Three provisions were narrowed to specific statistical numbers, so other paper towels (`4818.90.00.10`), control panels (`8537.10.91.80`) and fishing rods (`9507.10.00.80`) dropped out.

### The Section 232 exemption now covers dairy only

Under Revision 17, heading `9903.03.15` exempted Section 232 goods from all three Section 338 headings. Revision 19 changed note 51(c) so the exemption applies to `9903.03.13` only.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Section 232 goods now pay both">
From September 15, 2026, a Canadian product on list (b)(1) or (b)(3) that also pays Section 232 (steel or aluminum goods, autos, wood or metal furniture) pays the 50% on top. Several of the newly added lines are exactly those goods.
</Callout>

The civil aircraft exemption, `9903.03.16`, didn't change.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Some of these products are banned from September 29, 2026">
Proclamations 11061, 11062 and 11063 ban imports of certain Canadian alcoholic beverages, dairy products and motor vehicle products, listed in their annexes, from 12:01 a.m. eastern time on September 29, 2026. Banned goods can't be entered at any rate of duty. Goods imported before September 29 but not yet entered for consumption still pay the 50% Section 338 duty.
</Callout>

## What it means for importers

**On $10,000 of Canadian steel furniture** under `9403.20.00.82`:

| Entered | Headings | Additional duty |
|---|---|---|
| September 10, 2026 | Section 232 at 25% + `9903.03.15` exemption | $2,500 |
| September 20, 2026 | Section 232 at 25% + `9903.03.14` 50% | $7,500 |

Same product, same supplier, three times the added duty. If you import from Canada, run your HTS lines against the new lists, especially furniture, metal structures and fittings, and paper. Then check which of them already pay Section 232, because those are the lines where the cost jumped the most.
