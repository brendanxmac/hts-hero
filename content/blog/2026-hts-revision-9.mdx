2026 HTS Revision 9 caps the Section 232 tariff on Taiwan auto parts, upholstered wood furniture, kitchen cabinets and vanities at 15% including the regular duty, down from an extra 25%, and exempts certified Taiwan civil aircraft components from the metals duties. Everything is effective May 1, 2026, four weeks before the revision was published. It brings Taiwan into the deal structure that Japan, the EU and South Korea already had, carrying out the Section 232 terms of the January 15, 2026 Taiwan-U.S. investment MOU.

## What changed for Taiwan in Revision 9

### Taiwan auto parts: 15% including the regular duty

New U.S. note 33(u) and four new headings set the treatment for passenger vehicle and light truck parts that are products of Taiwan:

| Heading | Parts covered | Regular duty | Section 232 result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `9903.94.66` | Note 33(g) parts | 15% or more | No extra duty |
| `9903.94.67` | Note 33(g) parts | Under 15% | Topped up to 15% |
| `9903.94.68` | Note 33(r) parts | 15% or more | No extra duty |
| `9903.94.69` | Note 33(r) parts | Under 15% | Topped up to 15% |

Headings `9903.94.05` and `9903.94.07`, the general 25% auto parts headings, now list the Taiwan headings as exceptions. Under note 33(u), Taiwan parts entered under these headings also don't pay the Section 232 metals duties or the wood duties.

### Taiwan wood products: 15%

New heading `9903.76.24` covers wood products of Taiwan in subdivisions (d) and (f) of U.S. note 37, at **15%**. Those are upholstered wood furniture and kitchen cabinets and vanities. Notes 37(c) and (e) now exclude Taiwan from `9903.76.02` and `9903.76.03`, the 25% headings.

### Taiwan civil aircraft components: exempt from metals duties

New note 35(c) says the duties in `9903.82.02` and `9903.82.04` to `9903.82.19` don't apply to civil aircraft components of Taiwan that meet the criteria of General Note 6 and are classified in the listed provisions. New heading `9903.96.03` ("No change" in every column) is how you claim it.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Effective before publication">
The changes apply from May 1, 2026, but Revision 9 wasn't published until May 28. Taiwan goods entered from May 1 to May 27 at the old 25% rates may have overpaid.
</Callout>

### Cross-references and wording

- The Section 122 exemption in note 2(aa)(v) now lists `9903.94.66` to `9903.94.69` and `9903.76.24`.
- Note 39(a) keeps semiconductors under `9903.79.01` clear of the new Taiwan auto parts headings.
- UK headings `9903.94.31`, `9903.94.32` and `9903.96.01` now read "on or after June 30, 2025". That's a wording fix with no duty effect.

## Taiwan tariffs before and after May 1, 2026

**On $10,000 of Taiwan goods**, before and after May 1, 2026:

| Product | Before May 1 | From May 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Auto part, 2.5% regular duty | `9903.94.05`: 2.5% + 25% = $2,750 | `9903.94.67`: 15% total = $1,500 |
| Auto part, 20% regular duty | `9903.94.05`: 20% + 25% = $4,500 | `9903.94.66`: 20% only = $2,000 |
| Upholstered wood seat, free regular duty | `9903.76.02`: 25% = $2,500 | `9903.76.24`: 15% = $1,500 |
| Steel tube under [`7304.31.30`](/hts/7304.31.30.00) (free regular duty), certified civil aircraft component | `9903.82.02`: 50% = $5,000 | `9903.96.03`: $0 |

If you import auto parts, furniture or cabinets from Taiwan, check that your entries since May 1 use the new headings. For aircraft components, keep the General Note 6 documentation on file.

## What changed after Revision 9

- **June 8, 2026:** [Revision 10](/blog/2026-hts-revision-10) listed Taiwan in new heading `9903.82.22`, which caps Section 232 metals duty on mobile industrial equipment such as forklifts and earthmovers at 15%.
- **July 24, 2026:** [Revision 13](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13) added a Section 301 forced-labor duty for Taiwan that brings goods to 10% in total including the regular duty (`9903.05.75` and `9903.05.76`), with Taiwan-specific exemption lists.
