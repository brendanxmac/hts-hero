Revision 14 brings in the Section 232 tariff on pharmaceuticals, under Proclamation 11020. The headline rate is steep: 100% in total for a patented drug. Most of the detail is in who pays less.

## What changed

### Which products are covered

The tariff applies to articles classified in the provisions listed in U.S. note 40(c): **131 ten-digit statistical lines** in chapters 29 and 30, covering active ingredients, hormones, antibiotics, blood products and medicaments. Headings `9903.04.60` to `9903.04.69` are **mutually exclusive**, so each product enters under exactly one. A pharmaceutical article falls under `9903.04.60` unless another heading applies.

### The rates

| Heading | Applies to | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| `9903.04.60` | Patented pharmaceuticals (the default) | 100% in total, including the regular duty |
| `9903.04.62` | Products of Japan, the EU, South Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein | 15% in total, including the regular duty |
| `9903.04.63` | Products of the United Kingdom | +10% as published (see below) |
| `9903.04.64` | Companies with an approved onshoring plan | +20% |

The duties apply in addition to any special rate, so a free trade agreement claim doesn't remove them. Column 2 goods pay no additional pharmaceutical duty.

### The exemptions

These headings carry no pharmaceutical duty:

- `9903.04.61`: products imported for companies identified by the Secretary of Commerce, entered before September 29, 2026;
- `9903.04.65`: companies with an onshoring plan and a Most-Favored-Nation pricing agreement;
- `9903.04.66`: listed specialty products, including orphan drugs, nuclear medicines, plasma-derived products, fertility treatments and cell and gene therapies;
- `9903.04.67`: generic pharmaceuticals;
- `9903.04.68`: products whose active ingredient is a product of the United States, packaged in dosage form;
- `9903.04.69`: articles in the listed provisions that aren't pharmaceutical articles.

<Callout tone="primary" title="UK rate cut to zero, back to July 31">
Revision 14 published the UK rate as +10%. [Revision 15](/blog/2026-hts-revision-15) changed `9903.04.63` to +0% with the same July 31 start date, so UK patented pharmaceuticals pay no additional duty under it.
</Callout>

### Patented drugs drop out of the Brazil and forced-labor duties

Notes 50(a)(vi)(8) and 52(f)(8) now list patented pharmaceuticals under `9903.04.60` to `9903.04.66` as Section 232 articles. From July 31, those products are exempt from Brazil's 25% (`9903.05.07`) and from the Section 301 forced-labor country rates (`9903.05.90`). Generics, U.S.-ingredient products and non-pharmaceutical articles aren't on that list, so the country rate still applies to them.

## What it means for importers

**On $10,000 of a listed pharmaceutical ingredient** under `2918.99.30.00` (base rate free), entered August 1, 2026:

| Origin and status | Headings | Total duty |
|---|---|---|
| India, entered July 30 (before the tariff) | `9903.05.44`: 10% | $1,000 |
| India, patented | `9903.04.60`: 100% | $10,000 |
| Brazil, patented | `9903.04.60`, Brazil and forced-labor rates exempt | $10,000 |
| Germany, patented | `9903.04.62`: 15% in total | $1,500 |
| India, patented, onshoring plan | `9903.04.64`: +20% | $2,000 |
| India, generic | `9903.04.67` + `9903.05.44`: 10% | $1,000 |

Under note 40(a), a pharmaceutical article falls under the 100% heading unless another heading applies. If you import anything in chapters 29 or 30, check your lines against note 40(c), then work out which of the ten headings fits each product and what documents you'll need to support it.
