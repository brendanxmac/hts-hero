2026 HTS Revision 17 brings Section 338 into the tariff schedule. Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 lets the President add duties of up to 50% on goods from a country that discriminates against U.S. commerce. Proclamations 11046, 11047 and 11048 use it against Canada: a flat 50% on three lists of Canadian products, from August 22, 2026. That date falls two days before Revision 17 took effect, so entries on August 22 and 23 are covered too.

## What changed

### Three product lists, one 50% rate

New U.S. note 51 to Chapter 99 sets out the products. Each list has its own heading, and all three are 50%:

| Heading | List | Products | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `9903.03.12` | Note 51(b)(1), 63 provisions | Beer, wine and spirits, cosmetics, wood, paper, sporting goods | + 50% |
| `9903.03.13` | Note 51(b)(2), 52 provisions | Dairy and other food products | + 50% |
| `9903.03.14` | Note 51(b)(3), 439 provisions | Goods across Chapters 4 to 97 | + 50% |

The duties apply only to products of Canada. The Column 2 rate doesn't change.

The three proclamations, signed July 20, 2026, first set August 19 as the start date. Proclamation 11056, signed August 18, suspended the duties for three days while talks with Canada continued, which is why they start on August 22.

### The 50% applies even under USMCA

Note 51(a) says the 50% applies even when special tariff treatment is claimed. A USMCA-qualifying Canadian wine still pays the full Section 338 duty. The duties also stack with any other additional duty in Chapter 99.

<Callout tone="warning" title="A USMCA claim still matters">
Claiming USMCA can still remove other duties, like the 10% country rate on the wine below. The 50% stays either way.
</Callout>

### Exemptions: Section 232 goods and civil aircraft

- `9903.03.15`: goods subject to Section 232 duties, using the same list of Section 232 headings found elsewhere in Chapter 99, patented pharmaceuticals included. These pay their Section 232 duty and no Section 338 duty.
- `9903.03.16`: civil aircraft articles of Canada (excluding military and unmanned aircraft) that meet general note 6, in the listed provisions.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Some of these products are banned from September 29, 2026">
Proclamations 11061, 11062 and 11063 ban imports of certain Canadian products listed in their annexes from 12:01 a.m. eastern time on September 29, 2026: many beers, wines and spirits (some only when packaged for consumers), whey, molasses and non-alcoholic beer, and motorcycles over 800 cc. CBP rejects any entry that includes them ([CSMS #70050970](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/42ce49a)). Goods imported before September 29 but not yet entered for consumption can still be entered, and they pay the 50% Section 338 duty.
</Callout>

## What it means for importers

**On $10,000 of Canadian wine** under [`2204.21.20`](/hts/2204.21.20.00), entered before the September 29 ban:

| Entered | Headings | Additional duty |
|---|---|---|
| August 21, 2026 | No Section 338 duty | Country rate only |
| August 22, 2026 | `9903.03.12` 50% + 10% country rate | $6,000 |
| August 22, 2026, USMCA claimed | `9903.03.12` 50% | $5,000 |
| Same wine from France | No Section 338 duty | Unaffected |

From September 29, 2026, this wine is on the Proclamation 11061 ban list, so it can't be entered at all.

If you import from Canada, check every HTS line against the three note 51(b) lists, and against the ban annexes. A line can be on a list and still be exempt if it's a Section 232 good, but that exemption didn't last long: [Revision 19](/blog/2026-hts-revision-19) limited it to dairy products from September 15, 2026, and added more products to the lists. For how the 50% combines with Section 232 and the country rates, see [US tariff stacking rules](/blog/us-tariff-stacking-rules), and for every 2026 revision, the [2026 HTS revisions hub](/blog/2026-hts-revisions).
