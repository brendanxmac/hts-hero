<Callout tone="primary" title="Updated October 6, 2026">
This tariff is no longer collected. It was removed on February 7, 2026, two weeks before the Supreme Court ended the other IEEPA tariffs. We've kept the original breakdown for anyone reconciling 2025 and early-2026 entries, and added how it ended and what Indian goods pay now.
</Callout>

The 25% US tariff on Indian goods over India's purchases of Russian oil applied to entries from **August 27, 2025** through **February 6, 2026**, under HTS heading `9903.01.84`. An executive order removed it from February 7, 2026. Today, most goods from India pay a 10% Section 301 forced-labor duty on top of the base rate, and products covered by Section 232 pay their Section 232 duty instead.

## What the Russian oil tariff on India was

Executive Order 14329, signed August 6, 2025, announced an additional tariff on goods from India, citing India's purchases of Russian oil. CBP put it into effect from **12:01 a.m. EDT on August 27, 2025**: most imports from India paid an extra **25%**.

This came on top of the 25% reciprocal tariff India already faced, which put a lot of Indian goods at 50% in additional duty before counting the base rate.

## The HTS headings: 9903.01.84 to 9903.01.89

The tariff was added to Chapter 99, Subchapter III, under U.S. note 2(z): one heading for the tariff and five for its exemptions.

| Heading | What it covered |
|---|---|
| `9903.01.84` | The 25% additional duty on articles of India |
| `9903.01.85` | Goods loaded and in transit before August 27, 2025 and entered before September 17, 2025 |
| `9903.01.86` | Goods on the reciprocal tariff's exemption list (U.S. note 2(v)(iii)) |
| `9903.01.87` | Steel, aluminum, copper, passenger vehicles, light trucks and their parts subject to Section 232 |
| `9903.01.88` | Donations, such as food, clothing and medicine |
| `9903.01.89` | Informational materials, such as publications, films and recordings |

The Section 232 exemption mattered most in practice. Steel, aluminum, copper and vehicles from India that already paid Section 232 didn't pay the extra 25% on top.

## When the India tariff ended: February 7, 2026

On February 6, 2026, the US and India released a joint statement on a framework for an interim trade agreement. The same day, **Executive Order 14384** removed the Russian oil tariff, citing India's commitment to stop importing Russian oil, directly or indirectly. Goods of India entered on or after **12:01 a.m. EST on February 7, 2026** no longer paid it, and the order terminated headings `9903.01.84` to `9903.01.89` and U.S. note 2(z).

The order also tells the Commerce Department to monitor India's oil imports and recommend whether to bring the tariff back if India resumes buying Russian oil.

Two weeks later, on **February 20, 2026**, the Supreme Court held in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that IEEPA doesn't authorize tariffs. That ended India's remaining IEEPA duty, the reciprocal tariff, and CBP stopped collecting IEEPA tariffs from **February 24, 2026**.

## What US tariffs apply to Indian goods now

After the IEEPA tariffs ended, goods from India paid:

| Period | Additional tariff on most Indian goods |
|---|---|
| February 24 to July 23, 2026 | 10% Section 122 surcharge |
| From July 24, 2026 | 10% Section 301 forced-labor duty (`9903.05.44`) |

Both come on top of the HTS base rate. Most goods that pay a Section 232 duty (on metals, autos and parts, patented pharmaceuticals and other listed products) pay that instead, because the forced-labor duty switches off for them under heading `9903.05.90`. [2026 HTS Revision 13](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13) covers the switch from Section 122 to the forced-labor duties, and our guide to [US tariff stacking rules](/blog/us-tariff-stacking-rules) explains how they combine. To see what a specific product from India pays today, run its HTS code through the [duty calculator](/duty-calculator).

## If you imported from India during this period

Entries from August 27, 2025 through February 6, 2026 that reported `9903.01.84` paid an IEEPA duty. CBP is handling IEEPA refunds through CAPE declarations filed in the ACE Portal (see [CBP's IEEPA duty refund page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)). Check with your customs broker on the status of your entries, and keep your entry summaries and HTS classifications together.
