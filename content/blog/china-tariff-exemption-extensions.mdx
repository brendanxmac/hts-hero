<Callout tone="warning" title="Updated October 6, 2026: expiry is close">
These exclusions now expire on **November 9, 2026**. If your products rely on them, it's worth planning for the Section 301 duty coming back in case USTR doesn't extend them again.
</Callout>

## What these headings do

The Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods are split across lists, each with its own Chapter 99 heading (`9903.88.01`, `9903.88.02`, `9903.88.03` and so on). Over the years, USTR has excluded specific products from those tariffs. Two headings carry the exclusions that are still alive:

- `9903.88.69`: exclusions listed in U.S. note 20(vvv) to Subchapter III of Chapter 99
- `9903.88.70`: exclusions listed in U.S. note 20(www)

When a product matches one of these exclusions, the importer reports the exclusion heading instead of the Section 301 heading, and the 301 duty doesn't apply. The exclusion descriptions are often narrower than the HTS subheading they sit under, so a product can share a subheading with an excluded item and still owe the full tariff.

## The extension timeline

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| August 31, 2025 | Original expiry date for `9903.88.69` |
| September 1, 2025 | Both extended to November 29, 2025 |
| November 1, 2025 | US–China deal announces a further extension of the 178 exclusions |
| December 1, 2025 | USTR publishes the extension to November 9, 2026 |
| November 9, 2026 | Current expiry, 11:59 p.m. EDT |

## What to do before November 9

1. **List every product that uses `9903.88.69` or `9903.88.70`** on its entries.
2. **Work out the duty without the exclusion.** That's usually the base rate plus 25% for products on Lists 1 to 3.
3. **Watch for a USTR notice.** Extensions have tended to arrive close to the deadline, so don't assume either outcome.

A tariff change alert on your product catalog takes care of step 3 for you: when an HTS revision changes the duty on one of your products, you hear about it the same day.
