<Callout tone="warning" title="Updated October 6, 2026: expiry is close">
These exclusions expire at 11:59 p.m. ET on **November 9, 2026**, and USTR hasn't announced another extension yet. If your products rely on them, plan for the Section 301 duty coming back on entries from November 10.
</Callout>

The 178 remaining Section 301 China tariff exclusions, claimed under HTS headings `9903.88.69` and `9903.88.70`, are extended until **11:59 p.m. ET on November 9, 2026**. USTR set that date in a Federal Register notice published December 1, 2025, after the US-China deal of November 1, 2025. If they lapse, excluded products go back to paying 25% (Lists 1 to 3) or 7.5% (List 4A).

## What the 9903.88.69 and 9903.88.70 exclusions do

The Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods are split across lists, each with its own Chapter 99 heading (`9903.88.01`, `9903.88.02`, `9903.88.03` and so on). Over the years, USTR has excluded specific products from those tariffs. Two headings carry the exclusions that are still alive:

- `9903.88.69`: 164 product-specific exclusions listed in U.S. note 20(vvv) to Subchapter III of Chapter 99
- `9903.88.70`: 14 exclusions for solar manufacturing equipment, listed in U.S. note 20(www)

The HTS itself confirms these are the only ones left: a compiler's note to U.S. note 20 says only subdivisions (vvv) and (www) are now in effect.

When a product matches one of these exclusions, the importer reports the exclusion heading instead of the Section 301 heading, and the 301 duty doesn't apply. The exclusion descriptions are often narrower than the HTS subheading they sit under, so a product can share a subheading with an excluded item and still owe the full tariff.

<Callout tone="primary" title="Updated statistical numbers from July 1, 2026">
Some exclusions are written around 10-digit statistical numbers. When the HTS renumbered certain pump parts and plastic articles on July 1, 2026, the `9903.88.69` list picked up the new numbers. [2026 HTS Revision 18](/blog/2026-hts-revision-18) has the details.
</Callout>

## What duty applies if the exclusions expire

Without the exclusion, a Chinese product pays its full Section 301 rate again. Here's how the duty builds for an excluded product from China:

| Duty | With the exclusion | After it expires |
|---|---|---|
| HTS base rate | Applies | Applies |
| Section 301 list duty | None | 25% (Lists 1 to 3) or 7.5% (List 4A) |
| Section 301 forced-labor duty (`9903.05.31`) | 12.5% | 12.5% |
| Section 232, if the product is covered | Applies | Applies |

The forced-labor duty that started on July 24, 2026 applies to products of China whether or not they use a 301 exclusion, though it switches off for goods that pay a Section 232 duty. [2026 HTS Revision 13](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13) covers it, and our guide to [US tariff stacking rules](/blog/us-tariff-stacking-rules) explains how Section 301 China combines with everything else.

## What to do before November 9, 2026

1. **List every product that uses `9903.88.69` or `9903.88.70`** on its entries.
2. **Work out the duty without the exclusion.** That's usually the base rate plus 25% for products on Lists 1 to 3, plus the forced-labor duty. The [duty calculator](/duty-calculator) shows the full stack for any HTS code from China.
3. **Watch for a USTR notice.** Extensions have tended to arrive close to the deadline, so don't assume either outcome.

A tariff change alert on your product catalog takes care of step 3 for you. Add your products to the [Tariff Tracker](/tariff-tracker) and when an HTS revision changes the duty on one of them, you hear about it the same day.
