## How HTS revisions work

The US International Trade Commission (USITC) publishes the Harmonized Tariff Schedule once a year as a Basic Edition, then revises it whenever the law changes. In 2026 that's been often: a new proclamation, a Federal Register notice from USTR, or a technical correction each produces a revision.

Every revision comes with a **change record** that lists what changed. Most of the changes that move money are in Chapter 99, where the additional tariffs live. A revision can add tariffs, end them, change their rates, or change which products and countries they cover.

<Callout tone="primary" title="The entry date decides">
The duty on an import is set by the rules in force on its entry date. To check what you should have paid on a past entry, you need the revision that was in force that day.
</Callout>
