The US International Trade Commission (USITC) has published 20 revisions of the 2026 Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) so far, Revision 1 on January 16, 2026 through Revision 20 on September 28, 2026. As of October 6, 2026, Revision 20 is current. The table below lists every revision, the dates it was in force, and a link to our breakdown of what it changed.

## How HTS revisions work

The USITC publishes the Harmonized Tariff Schedule once a year as a Basic Edition, then revises it whenever the law changes. In 2026 that's been often: a new proclamation, a Federal Register notice from USTR, or a technical correction each produces a revision.

Every revision comes with a **change record** that lists what changed. Most of the changes that move money are in Chapter 99, where the additional tariffs live. A revision can add tariffs, end them, change their rates, or change which products and countries they cover.

<Callout tone="primary" title="The entry date decides">
The duty on an import is set by the rules in force on its entry date. To check what you should have paid on a past entry, you need the revision that was in force that day. A change can also start before or after the revision that publishes it: Revision 17's Canada duties, for example, applied from August 22, two days before the revision itself.
</Callout>

## The biggest 2026 HTS revisions

- [Revision 12](/blog/2026-hts-revision-12): a new 25% Section 301 tariff on products of Brazil.
- [Revision 13](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13): Section 122 ended and Section 301 forced-labor duties of 10% or 12.5% began.
- [Revision 14](/blog/2026-hts-revision-14): the Section 232 pharmaceutical tariff, up to 100% on patented drugs.
- [Revision 16](/blog/2026-hts-revision-16): a Section 201 safeguard on quartz surface products.
- [Revision 17](/blog/2026-hts-revision-17) and [Revision 19](/blog/2026-hts-revision-19): a 50% Section 338 tariff on listed Canadian goods, then wider lists that stack with Section 232.
- [Revision 18](/blog/2026-hts-revision-18): Section 232 tariffs of 25% or 100% on drones.
