## 2026 at a glance

2026 has been a busy year for US tariffs. Here are the changes that moved the most money, in order:

| Date | What changed |
|---|---|
| Jan 1 | **Section 301 China increases:** 25% on graphite, permanent magnets and non-EV lithium-ion batteries; 50% on textile face masks; 100% on rubber medical gloves |
| Feb 24 | **IEEPA tariffs end** (reciprocal and fentanyl), along with the EU, Japan and Korea deal headings built on them. **Section 122 10% surcharge starts** on nearly all imports |
| Apr 6 | **Section 232 metals restructured:** steel, aluminum and copper duties move to the full value of the goods, under a new `9903.82` family of headings |
| Apr 23 | USMCA steel and aluminum melted in Canada or Mexico, within authorized quantities: 25% instead of 50% |
| May 1 | **Taiwan deal:** 15% all-in on auto parts and wood furniture; civil aircraft components exempt from metals |
| Jun 8 | **Metals overhaul:** many machinery parts move to 15% all-in; new rules for mobile industrial equipment; U.S.-melted threshold lowered from 95% to 85% |
| Jul 22 | **Section 301 Brazil:** 25% on Brazilian goods, with exemptions |
| Jul 24 | **Section 122 ends. Section 301 forced labor starts:** 12.5% on 38 countries including China and Vietnam, 10% on 17 including Canada, Mexico and India, all-in rates for the EU, Taiwan, Japan, Korea and Switzerland |
| Jul 31 | **Section 232 pharmaceuticals:** patented drugs up to 100% all-in; 15% all-in for the EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein; generics exempt |
| Aug 15 | **Section 201 quartz safeguard:** 25% in quota, 50% over, stepping down each year until 2030 |
| Aug 22 | **Section 338 Canada:** 50% on listed Canadian products such as alcohol, dairy, wood and paper, even under USMCA |
| Sep 3 | **Section 232 drones:** 100%, or 25% without thermal imaging; 15% all-in for allies |
| Sep 15 | **Section 338 Canada expanded** to furniture, lamps, metal structures, cheese and boats, and most Section 232 goods now pay it on top |
| Sep 29 | **Pharmaceutical list updated** (131 to 149 products); new exemption for clinical trials and R&D |
| Sep 29 | **Canadian import bans:** certain alcoholic beverages, dairy products and motor vehicle products from Canada can no longer be imported (Proclamations 11061 to 11063) |
| Oct 1 | **New MPF limits** for fiscal year 2027: minimum $34.58, maximum $670.86 |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Coming up: November 10, 2026">
The 178 Section 301 China exclusions under `9903.88.69` and `9903.88.70` expire at 11:59 p.m. EDT on November 9, 2026, unless USTR extends them again. Products that rely on them go back to their Section 301 rate, often 25%. [More on the exclusions](/blog/china-tariff-exemption-extensions).
</Callout>

## How to read the table

- **New** is a new Chapter 99 heading. Red means a new duty; green means a new exemption.
- **Rate change** shows the rate before and after. Red if it went up, green if it went down.
- **Coverage** means the list of HTS codes a heading covers changed, so products moved in or out.
- **Terms** means the heading's conditions, countries or exemptions changed without a rate change.
- **Ended** means the heading no longer applies after that date.

A change to one heading can move the duty on thousands of products. If you import, the useful question is which of *your* products a change touches, and by how much.
