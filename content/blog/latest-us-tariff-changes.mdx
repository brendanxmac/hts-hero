The latest US tariff changes, as of October 6, 2026: on September 29, 2026, HTS Revision 20 expanded the Section 232 pharmaceutical list and added an exemption for clinical trials and R&D, and imports of certain Canadian alcoholic beverages, whey, molasses and motorcycles over 800 cc were banned ([details in Revision 17](/blog/2026-hts-revision-17)). New Merchandise Processing Fee limits started October 1. Below is every 2026 change that moved real money, then a full table of every dated Chapter 99 change, generated from HTS Hero's tariff data.

## 2026 US tariff changes at a glance

2026 has been a busy year for US tariffs. Here are the changes that moved the most money, in order:

| Date | What changed |
|---|---|
| Jan 1 | **Section 301 China increases:** 25% on graphite, permanent magnets and non-EV lithium-ion batteries; 50% on textile face masks; 100% on rubber medical gloves |
| Feb 24 | **IEEPA tariffs end** (reciprocal and fentanyl), along with the EU, Japan and Korea deal headings built on them. **Section 122 10% surcharge starts** on nearly all imports |
| Apr 6 | **Section 232 metals restructured** (Proclamation 11021): steel, aluminum and copper duties move to the full customs value of the goods, regardless of metal content, under a new `9903.82` family of headings |
| Apr 23 | USMCA steel and aluminum melted in Canada or Mexico, within authorized quantities: 25% instead of 50% |
| May 1 | **[Taiwan deal](/blog/2026-hts-revision-9):** 15% all-in on auto parts and wood furniture; civil aircraft components exempt from metals |
| Jun 8 | **[Metals overhaul](/blog/2026-hts-revision-10)** (Proclamation 11032): many machinery and equipment parts move to lower 10% and 15% treatments; new rules for mobile industrial equipment; U.S.-melted threshold lowered from 95% to 85% |
| Jul 22 | **[Section 301 Brazil](/blog/2026-hts-revision-12):** 25% on Brazilian goods, with exemptions |
| Jul 24 | **Section 122 ends. [Section 301 forced labor](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13) starts:** 12.5% on 38 economies including China and Vietnam, 10% on 17 including Canada, Mexico and India, all-in rates for the EU, Taiwan, Japan, Korea and Switzerland |
| Jul 31 | **[Section 232 pharmaceuticals](/blog/2026-hts-revision-14):** patented drugs up to 100% all-in; 15% all-in for the EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein; [0% for the UK](/blog/2026-hts-revision-15); generics exempt |
| Aug 15 | **[Section 201 quartz safeguard](/blog/2026-hts-revision-16):** 25% in quota, 50% over, stepping down each year until it ends in August 2030 |
| Aug 22 | **[Section 338 Canada](/blog/2026-hts-revision-17):** 50% on listed Canadian products such as alcohol, dairy, wood and paper, even under USMCA |
| Sep 3 | **[Section 232 drones](/blog/2026-hts-revision-18):** 100%, or 25% without thermal imaging; 15% all-in for allies when critical components come from the US or allies |
| Sep 15 | **[Section 338 Canada expanded](/blog/2026-hts-revision-19)** to furniture, lamps, metal structures, cheese and boats, and most Section 232 goods now pay it on top |
| Sep 29 | **[Pharmaceutical list updated](/blog/2026-hts-revision-20)** (131 to 149 statistical lines); new exemption for clinical trials and R&D (`9903.04.70`); exemption for companies identified by Commerce ends |
| Sep 29 | **Canadian import bans:** certain Canadian beers, wines and spirits, whey, molasses, non-alcoholic beer and motorcycles over 800 cc can no longer be imported (Proclamations 11061 to 11063) |
| Oct 1 | **New MPF limits** for fiscal year 2027: minimum $34.58, maximum $670.86 |

## How to read the tariff changes table

- **New** is a new Chapter 99 heading. Red means a new duty; green means a new exemption.
- **Rate change** shows the rate before and after. Red if it went up, green if it went down.
- **Coverage** means the list of HTS codes a heading covers changed, so products moved in or out.
- **Terms** means the heading's conditions, countries or exemptions changed without a rate change.
- **Ended** means the heading no longer applies after that date.

A change to one heading can move the duty on thousands of products. If you import, the useful question is which of *your* products a change touches, and by how much. For how these tariffs combine on a single product, see [US tariff stacking rules](/blog/us-tariff-stacking-rules), and for each revision in detail, the [2026 HTS revisions hub](/blog/2026-hts-revisions).
