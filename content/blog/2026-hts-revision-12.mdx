Revision 12 adds one new tariff, and it's a big one for anyone sourcing from Brazil: 25% on top of everything else, from July 22, 2026. The structure copies the Section 122 surcharge clause for clause, so the exemptions will look familiar.

## What changed

### 25% on all products of Brazil

New U.S. note 50 and heading `9903.05.01` add **25%** to the regular duty on "all products of Brazil". Two details matter:

- **It stacks.** Goods under `9903.05.01` are also subject to any other additional duty in the subchapter. On July 22 and 23, that meant Section 122's 10% as well.
- **Trade preferences don't remove it.** Goods that qualify for a special rate under general note 3(c)(i) still pay the 25%.

### Eight exemptions

| Heading | Exempts |
|---|---|
| `9903.05.02` | Goods loaded before 12:01 a.m. ET on July 22, 2026 and entered before 12:01 a.m. ET on July 29, 2026 |
| `9903.05.03` | Products in the subheadings listed in note 50(a)(ii), such as coffee |
| `9903.05.04` | 11 particular articles in note 50(a)(iii), including etrogs, açaí and some religious-use items |
| `9903.05.05` | Civil aircraft, engines, parts and ground flight simulators, as described in note 50(a)(iv) |
| `9903.05.06` | Articles for use in pharmaceutical applications, in the provisions listed in note 50(a)(v) |
| `9903.05.07` | Section 232 articles: metals, autos and parts, trucks and parts, wood products and semiconductors |
| `9903.05.08` | Donations, such as food, clothing and medicine, to relieve human suffering |
| `9903.05.09` | Informational materials, such as publications, films and recordings |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Not every metals heading counts">
The Section 232 exemption in note 50(a)(vi) lists the metals headings `9903.82.02` and `9903.82.04` to `9903.82.26`. An article on the metals lists entered under `9903.82.01` (no aluminum, steel or copper) pays no Section 232 duty, so it gets no exemption here and pays the 25%.
</Callout>

The pharmaceutical exemption depends on end use. A product classified in a listed provision qualifies only if it's actually for pharmaceutical applications, so be ready to support that.

## What it means for importers

**On $10,000 of cotton T-shirts** from Brazil under `6109.10.00.04` (base rate 16.5%):

| Entered | Additional duties | Total duty |
|---|---|---|
| July 21, 2026 | Section 122 10% | $2,650 |
| July 22, 2026 | Section 122 10% + Brazil 25% | $5,150 |
| July 25, 2026, as published in Revision 12 | Brazil 25% | $4,150 |
| July 25, 2026, after the forced-labor duty started | Brazil 25% + `9903.05.27` 12.5% | $5,400 |

The last row reflects [Revision 13](/blog/2026-hts-revision-13): Section 122 ended at the close of July 23, and Brazil's 12.5% Section 301 forced-labor rate started on July 24. Both changes fall inside Revision 12's window.

**On $10,000 of steel** under `7206.90.00.00` from Brazil, the 50% Section 232 duty applies and `9903.05.07` exempts the 25%. Total: $5,000.

If you import from Brazil, check each product against the note 50 lists before anything else. The list in note 50(a)(ii) alone runs to 864 subheadings, and an exemption there saves the full 25%.
