## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Revision** | 2026 HTS Revision 13 |
| **In force from** | July 28, 2026 |
| **Tariff changes effective** | July 24, 2026 |
| **Programs affected** | Section 122 (ended), Section 301 forced labor (new) |
| **Previous revision** | [Revision 12](/blog/2026-hts-revision-12) |
| **Next revision** | [Revision 14](/blog/2026-hts-revision-14) |

Revision 13 swaps one broad tariff for another. The 10% Section 122 surcharge ran out at the close of July 23, 2026, and the next day a set of country-by-country Section 301 duties took its place. The new duties started on July 24, while Revision 12 was still in force, so entries from July 24 to July 27 are affected too.

## What changed

### Section 122 expired

A compiler's note in Revision 13 records that U.S. note 2(aa) and headings `9903.03.01` to `9903.03.11` **expired at the close of July 23, 2026**. The surcharge had added 10% to products of all countries, with exemptions, since February 24, 2026.

### New Section 301 forced-labor duties

New U.S. note 52 and headings `9903.05.20` to `9903.05.84` add a duty on all products of the listed countries from **July 24, 2026**:

| Rate | Countries |
|---|---|
| +12.5% | 38 countries, including China, Vietnam, Brazil, Thailand, Australia, Singapore, the Philippines, Israel and Saudi Arabia |
| +10% | 17 countries, including Canada, Mexico, India, the United Kingdom, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia |
| 10% in total, including the regular duty | the EU and Taiwan |
| 12.5% in total, including the regular duty | Japan, South Korea and Switzerland |

Like the Brazil duty in [Revision 12](/blog/2026-hts-revision-12), these rates **stack** with other Chapter 99 duties and apply even when a preferential rate is claimed. Chinese goods pay the 12.5% on top of the existing Section 301 China duties. Brazilian goods pay it on top of Brazil's 25%.

For the "total" countries, note 52(k) uses the ad valorem equivalent of the regular duty: duty payable divided by customs value. So a Japanese product with a specific rate that works out to 5% gets 7.5% added.

### Exemptions

Headings `9903.05.85` to `9903.06.21` exempt:

- goods loaded before July 24, 2026 and entered before July 28, 2026 (`9903.05.85`);
- products listed in note 52(b) (`9903.05.86`) and 16 particular articles in note 52(c) (`9903.05.87`);
- civil aircraft (`9903.05.88`) and articles for pharmaceutical applications (`9903.05.89`);
- Section 232 articles (`9903.05.90`), donations (`9903.05.91`) and informational materials (`9903.05.92`);
- goods of Canada and Mexico entered free under the USMCA (`9903.05.93`, `9903.05.94`);
- CAFTA-DR textile and apparel goods (`9903.05.95`);
- country-specific lists for the UK, EU and Switzerland (`9903.05.96` to `9903.05.98`), and for Malaysia, Cambodia, Guatemala, El Salvador, Argentina, Bangladesh, Taiwan, Indonesia, Ecuador and Jordan (`9903.05.99` to `9903.06.21`).

<Callout tone="warning" title="Russia pays in Column 2 too">
Most country headings add nothing for Column 2 goods. Russia's heading, `9903.05.66`, is the exception: its Column 2 rate reads "+ 12.5%", so Russian goods pay the 12.5% on top of the Column 2 rate.
</Callout>

## What it means for importers

**On $10,000 of cotton T-shirts** under `6109.10.00.04` (base rate 16.5%):

| Origin | July 23, 2026 | July 24, 2026 onward |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnam | $2,650 (Section 122) | $2,900 (`9903.05.84`, 12.5%) |
| United Kingdom | $2,650 (Section 122) | $2,650 (`9903.05.81`, 10%) |
| Brazil | $5,150 (Section 122 + Brazil 25%) | $5,400 (Brazil 25% + `9903.05.27`, 12.5%) |

**On $10,000 of valves** under `8481.80.90.50`:

| Origin and base rate | Heading | Total duty |
|---|---|---|
| Germany, 4% | `9903.05.39`: topped up to 10% | $1,000 |
| Germany, 12% | `9903.05.38`: nothing added | $1,200 |
| Japan, 2% | `9903.05.49`: topped up to 12.5% | $1,250 |

For goods from the UK, Canada, Mexico or India, the headline rate is the same 10% that Section 122 charged. For the 38 countries at 12.5%, it's 2.5 points more. Check the country-specific exemption lists before you assume the rate applies: several of them are long, and Canadian and Mexican goods only escape it with a USMCA claim.
