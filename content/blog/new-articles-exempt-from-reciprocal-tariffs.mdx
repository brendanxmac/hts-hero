<Callout tone="primary" title="Updated October 6, 2026">
The reciprocal tariff ended after the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling on February 20, 2026. We've kept this list because it still matters for reconciling entries made before then, and because the lesson about partial exemptions applies to plenty of tariffs still in force.
</Callout>

## What changed in November 2025

On November 13, 2025, the US added **237 subheadings** to the list of goods exempt from the reciprocal tariff. Most were agricultural products the US doesn't produce in volume: tropical fruits, coffee, spices, juices and the like.

The interesting part was a group of **11 partial exemptions**. For these, the exemption covered one specific product inside an HTS subheading, and every other product in that subheading still paid.

## The 11 partial exemptions

| Subheading | Only this product was exempt |
|---|---|
| `0805.90.01` | Etrogs |
| `0811.90.80` | Tropical fruit, nesoi, frozen, whether or not previously steamed or boiled |
| `1404.90.90` | Date palm branches, myrtus branches or other vegetable material, for religious purposes only |
| `1905.90.10` | Bread, pastry, cakes, biscuits and similar baked products, nesoi, and puddings, for religious purposes only |
| `1905.90.90` | Bakers' wares, communion wafers, sealing wafers, rice paper and similar products, nesoi, for religious purposes only |
| `2008.99.21` | Acai |
| `2009.31.60` | Citrus juice of any single citrus fruit (other than orange, grapefruit or lime), Brix value not over 20, concentrated, unfermented, except lemon juice |
| `2009.89.70` | Coconut water or juice of acai |
| `2009.90.40` | Coconut water juice blends, not from concentrate, packaged for retail sale |
| `2106.90.99` | Acai preparations for the manufacture of beverages |
| `3301.29.51` | Essential oils other than those of citrus fruit, nesoi, for religious purposes only |

## Why partial exemptions matter

A 10-digit HTS code usually decides the duty. Partial exemptions break that rule: two products under the same code can pay different amounts depending on what they actually are.

That pattern didn't go away with the reciprocal tariff. Section 232 and Section 301 both have exemptions written around product descriptions and end uses, and they're easy to miss if you only look at the code. A classification that records *why* a product fits its code, with the description to back it up, is what lets you claim them with confidence.
